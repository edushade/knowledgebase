A certificate template is the **drawn document**: the artwork, the wording, where the
learner's name sits. It is separate from the
[certificate type](/docs/knowledgebase/certificates/certificate-types), which is the
rule for earning one.

Templates live in <Path>Settings > Certificate Templates</Path>.

## Making one

<Steps>

<Step>

### Name it and say what it is for

**Template name** is required. The description is a note for your team — "which
programmes print this".

</Step>

<Step>

### Choose its kind

A template belongs to a certificate type, so a completion certificate and an enrolment
letter are different templates.

</Step>

<Step>

### Draw it

Open the builder and lay out the document. See
[Designing a document](/docs/knowledgebase/certificates/designing-a-document) for how
the builder works.

You can **start from a blank page** or from the default design. Starting blank is a
decision the screen asks you to confirm, because there is no way back to what was there.

</Step>

<Step>

### Check it with a sample

**Download a sample** renders the design with example data, so you see the real thing
before a learner does. It can take a moment to be ready.

</Step>

</Steps>

## Assigning a template

| Where | What it sets |
|---|---|
| On the certificate type | The default for everything that grants this type. |
| On a programme's settings | An override for that programme only. |

A programme that names no design uses its type's default. That is what lets forty
courses share one design and one of them print its own.

## Before you change a live design

- **Certificates already issued keep the design they were issued on.** Changing a
  template does not rewrite documents already in learners' hands — which is right, and
  also means a mistake you fix today does not fix yesterday's.
- **Always download a sample** after a change. A field that overflows, or artwork that
  covers the name, is obvious on a sample and invisible in the editor.
- **Reset to the default design** puts the document back to what a new design starts
  from. Anything you have drawn is lost, and the screen says so before it does it.

## What to put on it

The essentials, in rough order of how much they matter:

1. **The learner's name**, large and correct.
2. **The programme**, named as learners and employers would recognise it.
3. **The certificate number.** Without it the certificate cannot be checked — see
   [Verifying certificates](/docs/knowledgebase/certificates/verifying-certificates).
4. **The date**, and the grade where your academy awards one.
5. **A signature and a seal**, where your institution uses them.

## Common questions

<Accordions type="single">

<Accordion title="A field is empty on the printed certificate.">
The data behind it is empty for that learner — no grade yet, no cohort code. A good
design says what to print when a field is empty rather than leaving a gap. See
[Designing a document](/docs/knowledgebase/certificates/designing-a-document).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can one programme print a different design from the rest?">
Yes — name a design on that programme's settings. It overrides the type's default.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I use a different design per batch?">
Designs are chosen on the programme. For a batch that genuinely needs its own, give it
its own certificate type and template.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="The sample will not download.">
It is still rendering. Wait a moment and try again — the screen tells you when it is not
ready yet.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I upload a PDF as the certificate?">
No. A certificate is drawn in the builder so that each learner's name, grade and number
can be filled in. A flat PDF cannot do that.
</Accordion>

</Accordions>
