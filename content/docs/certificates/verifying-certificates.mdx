An employer has a certificate with your academy's name on it and wants to know it is
real. Here is exactly what you can offer them today.

<Callout type="warn">
**There is no public verification page.** A third party cannot type a code into your
website and be told whether a certificate is genuine. The QR code a certificate can
carry currently leads to the course's page, not to a verification result.
</Callout>

<WhoCan roles="Admin" />

## What a certificate carries

| On the document | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Certificate number** | A unique number, built from your pattern. See [Issuing certificates](/docs/knowledgebase/certificates/issuing-certificates). |
| **Verification code** | A code that identifies this certificate. |
| **A QR code** | Optional, if the design includes one. |
| **The learner's name, programme, grade and dates** | The claim itself. |

The number and the code are what make a certificate checkable. A design that prints
neither leaves you nothing to check against.

## How verification works today

Somebody sends you a number or a code. You look it up:

1. Find the learner, in <Path>People</Path> or <Path>Enrollments</Path>.
2. The enrolment list carries **Certificate** and **Certificate Issued At**.
3. Check the number and the details on the document against what you hold.

It is a manual answer, and for most academies the volume makes that fine. Where it is
not, say so to the person asking rather than pointing them at a page that will not
help.

## Making certificates checkable

Three things, now, that make verification work when you are asked:

1. **Put the number on the document.** A design without the certificate number cannot
   be checked at all.
2. **Use a readable pattern.** `ES-{PROGRAM_CODE}-{COHORT_CODE}-{RANDOM:4}` tells you
   the programme and the batch before you look anything up.
3. **Set the pattern before you issue.** Certificates keep the number they were given,
   so a later change leaves two schemes in circulation.

## What learners can do

A learner can view and download their own certificate from their dashboard at any time,
and send the file on. That is the ordinary route — the file itself, from the person it
belongs to.

## Common questions

<Accordions type="single">

<Accordion title="Can I put a QR code on the certificate?">
A design can include one, and the data to drive it exists. Be aware of where it
currently leads — the course's page, not a verification result — before you tell anyone
it verifies the certificate.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Someone sent me a certificate that looks wrong.">
Check the number against your records. A number that does not exist, or exists against
a different learner or programme, is your answer.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I stop certificates being edited after download?">
No more than any PDF. The number is what makes a forgery checkable, which is the
argument for always printing it.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Is there an open badge or a credential standard?">
Not implemented. What exists is the certificate document, its number and its code.
</Accordion>
