title: "Issuing Certificates"
description: "How certificates go out on their own, how to issue one by hand, and how their numbers are built."

Most certificates issue themselves. The system checks after anything that could change
the answer — a grade recorded, a result released, a form submitted — and issues when
the gates are clear.

Sometimes you have to do it yourself.

<WhoCan roles="Admin" />

## Automatic issue

Nothing to do. When a learner clears all six gates, the certificate is created and
appears in their dashboard.

The gates, and why one is not issuing, are on
[Certificate requirements](/docs/knowledgebase/certificates/certificate-requirements).

## Issuing by hand

From an enrolment, **Issue Certificate**. This **bypasses the requirements entirely** —
it issues for a learner who has not met them.

That is the point. Use it for:

- The agreed exception, approved by someone who can approve it.
- Work completed outside the platform.
- A programme where the certificate is a judgement rather than a calculation.

Do not use it to work around a gate you could fix. A learner waiting on a release date
should wait; a learner waiting because the passing basis is wrong needs the basis
corrected, or everyone else will be stuck too.

<Callout type="warn">
A manual issue is recorded as any other action is, with who did it and when. Issue one
for the right reason and you will be glad of that record; issue one for the wrong
reason and so will somebody else.
</Callout>

## Certificate numbers

Every certificate carries a number, built from a pattern you set once in
<Path>Settings > Control Panel</Path> under **Certificate Number Generation**.

The pattern can use:

| Token | Becomes |
|---|---|
| `{PROGRAM_CODE}` | The programme's **Certificate Code**, or its slug if you left that empty. |
| `{COHORT_CODE}` | The batch's **Cohort Code**, or one worked out from its title. |
| `{YEAR:4}` | The year. |
| `{RANDOM:n}` | n random characters. |
| `{SERIAL}` | A running number. |

For example `ES-{PROGRAM_CODE}-{COHORT_CODE}-{RANDOM:4}` produces `ES-DS-C1-9K2X`.
Leave the pattern empty and `CERT-{YEAR:4}-{RANDOM:6}` is used.

<Callout type="info">
Set the pattern **before** you issue anything. Certificates already issued keep the
numbers they were given, so changing the pattern later leaves you with two schemes in
circulation.
</Callout>

Give each programme a short **Certificate Code** on its settings step, and each batch a
**Cohort Code**, and the numbers become readable: someone holding `ES-DS-C1-9K2X` can
be found without a lookup.

## What the learner gets

A certificate in their dashboard, which they can view and download. The design is
whichever one the programme or its type uses — see
[Certificate templates](/docs/knowledgebase/certificates/certificate-templates).

## Common questions

<Accordions type="single">

<Accordion title="Can I revoke a certificate?">
An issued certificate stands. That is why the gates are checked before issuing rather
than afterwards — and why the overall passing basis waits for everything to be marked.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I issue certificates to a whole batch at once?">
There is no bulk issue. If a batch has all passed, automatic issue should be handling
them — a whole batch waiting usually means a gate, not a missing button. See
[Certificate requirements](/docs/knowledgebase/certificates/certificate-requirements).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="A learner's name is wrong on their certificate.">
The certificate prints the name on their account. Correct the account and re-issue.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can a learner get a certificate for one semester?">
Not a completion certificate for the programme — a learner holding part of a programme
never qualifies. What you can give them is an
[enrolment certificate](/docs/knowledgebase/certificates/certificate-types), or a
[transcript](/docs/knowledgebase/progress-grading/transcripts) for the semester.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Where do I see who has one?">
The enrolment list has **Certificate** and **Certificate Issued At** columns, and
[Certificate analytics](/docs/knowledgebase/analytics/certificates) shows the same
across your academy.
</Accordion>

</Accordions>
