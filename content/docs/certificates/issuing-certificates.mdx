---
title: "Issuing Certificates"
description: "How certificates are awarded automatically, how to award or revoke one yourself, and what the learner sees."
---

A certificate can be awarded in two ways: **automatically**, when a learner meets the
requirements you set, or **manually**, when you award it yourself. This page covers
both, plus revoking and what the learner gets.

## Automatic issuing

Once you have [turned on requirements](/docs/knowledgebase/certificates/certificate-requirements),
the platform watches for the learner to meet them and awards the certificate on its own.

It checks the requirements whenever something relevant happens, such as:

- The learner **finishes** the course or learning path.
- The learner **submits** the required form.
- The learner's **review is approved**.

When that happens, the platform re-checks the requirements. If they are met (under your
**All** or **Any** setting), the certificate is awarded right away. If they are not met
yet, nothing happens until the learner does the remaining steps.

<Callout type="success">
A learner is never awarded the same certificate twice. Once it is issued, repeat events
will not create a duplicate.
</Callout>

## Awarding a certificate yourself

Sometimes you want to give a learner a certificate directly — for example, to recognise
work done outside the usual flow, or when you have left requirements off. You can award
a certificate to a learner by hand at any time, regardless of the automatic rules.

1. Open the learner's enrollment for the course or learning path.
2. Choose **Issue certificate**.
3. The certificate is created with a unique number and verification code, ready for the
   learner to view.

## Revoking a certificate

If a certificate was awarded by mistake, or should no longer stand, you can **revoke**
it. A revoked certificate is no longer valid and the learner can no longer use it.

## What the learner sees

- When a certificate is awarded, the learner can **view it in their account**.
- They can **download** it to keep, print, or share.
- It shows the course or path title, their grade, the completion date, the time they
  spent, the unique certificate number, and the verification code.

<Callout type="info">
Want anyone to be able to confirm a certificate is real? See
[Verifying certificates](/docs/knowledgebase/certificates/verifying-certificates).
</Callout>
