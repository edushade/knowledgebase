How a video behaves — whether a learner can drag the scrubber to the end, change the
speed, or see the source site's branding — is a setting, not a property of the video.

It is set academy-wide, and can be overridden per programme.

<WhoCan roles="Admin" />

## The two players

For YouTube and Vimeo videos you choose, per provider:

| Choice | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Managed player (custom player)** | The video plays inside your own player. Seeking, speed and keyboard are under your control, and the provider's branding and related videos are always hidden. |
| **YouTube / Vimeo default player** | Their player runs and controls everything. |

<Callout type="warn">
**The controls below apply to the managed player only.** With both providers on their
default player, the settings affect uploaded videos alone — the screen warns you when
that is the case.
</Callout>

## The controls

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Allow seeking** | Off locks the scrubber, so learners must watch through. |
| **Playback speed control** | Lets learners change the speed. |
| **Keyboard shortcuts** | Space and arrow keys in the player. |
| **Privacy-enhanced mode (YouTube)** | Uses YouTube's no-cookie address, so no tracking cookies are set until play. |

### About locking seeking

Turning **Allow seeking** off is how an academy stops learners dragging to the end of a
video to tick it off.

It is worth being honest about what it costs: a learner who wants to re-watch one
section has to sit through everything before it. For assessed material it is
defensible; for a reference video it makes the course worse.

A better answer for most courses is a sensible
[completion rule](/docs/knowledgebase/progress-grading/completion-rules) — 90% watched —
with seeking left on.

### Privacy-enhanced mode

Switch it on. It costs nothing, and it means YouTube sets no tracking cookies on your
learners until they actually press play — which matters if you ask for cookie consent.

## Per-programme override

**Customize video playback for this program**, on a course's settings step. Off, the
course follows your academy-wide settings; on, it has its own.

Use it for the one course where the rules genuinely differ — an assessed series where
seeking is locked, inside an academy where it is not.

## The YouTube limitation

The managed player cannot get around YouTube's "confirm you're not a bot" check. When
YouTube decides to show it, your learner sees it.

If reliable playback matters — a paid course, an exam-prep series — host the videos
yourself rather than embedding them. It costs more and it works.

## Where videos come from

| Source | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Self hosted** | Uploaded to your own library. Most control, no outside branding. |
| **YouTube** | Free to serve, plays well on weak connections, subject to the above. |
| **Vimeo** | Similar, fewer distractions. |

See [Lesson types](/docs/knowledgebase/courses/lesson-types).

## Common questions

<Accordions type="single">

<Accordion title="I turned off seeking and learners can still skip.">
Those videos are on the provider's default player, where your settings do not apply. Set
the provider to **Managed**.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Learners say a video will not play.">
If it is a YouTube video, the bot check is the usual cause, and it is outside your
control. Self-hosting is the fix.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Videos count as watched before they are.">
Check the lesson's **Duration**. Progress is measured against the figure you typed. See
[Lesson types](/docs/knowledgebase/courses/lesson-types).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Should I use the managed player?">
Yes, for anything assessed — it gives you the controls and hides the related videos that
take learners away. For a casual reference video it matters less.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can learners download videos?">
Media settings in <Path>Settings > Control Panel</Path> govern downloads, including
whether paid content may be downloaded. See
[Sharing and limits](/docs/knowledgebase/files/sharing-and-limits).
</Accordion>

</Accordions>
