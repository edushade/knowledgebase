---
title: "Alternative Assessments"
description: "Making a main exam and its makeup interchangeable, so that sitting either one satisfies the same slot."
---

A learner misses the final exam and sits the makeup. Without help, the system sees two
assessments: one missed and one passed. An alternative group tells it there is really
one.

**A group is one assessment slot.** Completing any member satisfies it — for progress,
for unlock order, and for the result.

<WhoCan roles="Admin, Instructor" />

## Making a group

Open the course builder and click **Alternative Groups**, at the top right.

<Steps>

<Step>

### Pick two or more assessments

They must belong to the **same course or batch**. Assessments are listed under the
programme they belong to, and once you pick one, the others lock — a group cannot
reach across two programmes, because it feeds one programme's grading.

Only ungrouped assessments are offered. One that already belongs to a group is hidden.

</Step>

<Step>

### Star the primary

One member is the **primary**. It is the one the slot is named by, and the one that
carries the slot's weight.

Make the main assessment the primary, not the makeup.

</Step>

<Step>

### Save

The group appears in the list of existing groups, and the assessments now behave as one
slot.

</Step>

</Steps>

## What changes

**Progress.** The slot counts once. A learner who sat the makeup is not short a lesson.

**Unlock order.** Anything waiting on the exam is satisfied by the makeup too. See
[Prerequisites](/docs/knowledgebase/enrollment-access/prerequisites).

**The result.** The slot's score is whatever the learner actually sat. The group is
counted exactly once.

**Weights.** The slot's weight sits on the primary. The other members appear in the
weights editor with a link back to their primary, and:

- their weight may be **at most** the primary's;
- it does **not** count towards the programme's 100% total.

See [Weighted grading](/docs/knowledgebase/progress-grading/weighted-grading).

**The transcript.** The row printed is the member the learner took.

## What happens to a learner who sits both

The slot takes one score. If a learner sits both the exam and its makeup, cap the
makeup's weight below the primary's so the alternative route cannot be worth more than
the original.

## Breaking a group up

Deleting a group makes its assessments separate again — each becomes its own slot. **No
quiz, assignment or learner data is deleted**; only the link between them.

Recalculate the programme's grades afterwards, because the number of slots has changed.

## Common questions

<Accordions type="single">

<Accordion title="No assessments are offered when I try to make a group.">
Every assessment in the programme is already in one. Members of other groups are hidden
so you cannot put one assessment in two places.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can a group span a course and its learning path?">
No. A group lives in one course or batch, because it feeds that programme's grading.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Which member does the transcript show?">
The one the learner actually took. A learner who sat the makeup sees the makeup.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Is this the same as a makeup assessment?">
A makeup is the *assessment*; an alternative group is what makes it count as the
original. Setting up a makeup without grouping it leaves you with a missed exam and an
extra quiz. See [Makeup assessments](/docs/knowledgebase/assessments/makeup-assessments).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can a group have three members?">
Yes — two or more. A main exam with two makeup sittings works.
</Accordion>

</Accordions>
