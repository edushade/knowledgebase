---
title: "Makeup Assessments"
description: "Running a second sitting for the learners who missed the first — and making it count as the same assessment."
---

Someone missed the exam. You run it again for them. The question is whether the system
sees one assessment sat twice, or two assessments of which each learner missed one.

Getting that right is two steps, and the second is the one people forget.

<WhoCan roles="Admin, Instructor" />

## Two ways to run a makeup

### The same quiz, a new window

If the paper can be reused, give the affected learners a
[reschedule](/docs/knowledgebase/assessments/reschedules): their own unlock and lock
times on the same quiz. Nothing else changes — same assessment, same slot, same weight.

This is the simpler route, and the right one when only a few learners are involved and
the questions have not leaked.

### A separate makeup paper, grouped with the original

If you need a different paper — because the first has been seen — build a second quiz
and then **group it with the original**.

<Steps>

<Step>

### Build the makeup quiz

A new quiz in the same course or batch, settled like the original: same attempts, same
time limit, same pass mark.

</Step>

<Step>

### Give it its own window

Add it to the course and set its drip schedule for the makeup sitting.

</Step>

<Step>

### Group it with the original

In the course builder, **Alternative Groups** → pick both assessments → **star the
original as the primary** → save.

</Step>

<Step>

### Cap its weight

In the weights editor the makeup appears beneath its primary. Its weight may be at most
the primary's, and does not count towards the programme's 100%. Cap it at or below the
original so the makeup route is never worth more.

</Step>

</Steps>

<Callout type="warn">
**Skipping step 3 is the mistake.** Without the group, the system sees two assessments.
Every learner who sat the original shows a missed makeup, every learner who sat the
makeup shows a missed exam, and both drag on the result if **Treat Ungraded Items as
Zero** is on.
</Callout>

## What grouping gives you

- **One slot.** Sitting either satisfies it — for progress, for unlock order, for the
  result.
- **The right row on the transcript.** The member the learner actually sat.
- **One weight.** Counted once, on the primary.

Full detail: [Alternative assessments](/docs/knowledgebase/assessments/alternative-assessments).

## Choosing between the two routes

| | **Reschedule** | **Separate makeup paper** |
|---|---|---|
| Effort | Minutes | An hour or two |
| New questions needed | No | Yes |
| Use when | A few learners, paper not seen | Many learners, or the paper has leaked |
| Extra step | None | Must be grouped |

## Common questions

<Accordions type="single">

<Accordion title="A learner sat both the exam and the makeup.">
The slot takes one score, from the member they took. Where both exist, cap the makeup's
weight below the primary's so the alternative route cannot be worth more.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can a makeup be a different kind of assessment?">
Yes — a group can hold a quiz and an assignment, as long as they live in the same
course or batch.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I group three sittings?">
Yes. Two or more members, one primary.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="The learner is still showing as missing the original.">
The group is not in place, or they are not in the group's programme. Open **Alternative
Groups** and check the original and the makeup are both in it.
</Accordion>

</Accordions>
