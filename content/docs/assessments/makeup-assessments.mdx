---
title: "Makeup & Alternative Assessments"
description: "Group a main exam with a makeup so a learner can take either one — and it counts only once toward the grade."
---

Sometimes you want to offer a learner a choice of two equivalent assessments — a main exam and
a makeup, or two versions of the same test. Makeup grouping lets you bundle them together so a
learner completes **any one** of them, and it counts once toward the grade. No double-counting,
no manual juggling.

## How it works

You group two or more interchangeable assessments into a single **slot**:

- A learner completes **any one** member of the slot to satisfy it.
- One member is the **primary** — it carries the grading weight in the final grade.
- The score used is from **whichever assessment the learner actually took** — so if they sat
  the makeup instead of the main exam, the makeup's score is what counts.

This means the slot is graded fairly: it never adds the scores together and never counts an
assessment twice.

## Example

You set a midterm exam and a midterm makeup as one slot, with the midterm as the primary:

- A learner who sits the **midterm** gets that score.
- A learner who missed it and sits the **makeup** gets the makeup score instead.
- Either way, the slot contributes **one** midterm grade to the final result.

## Rules for a group

- A group needs **at least two** members.
- Exactly **one** member is the primary.
- Each assessment can belong to **at most one** group within the same offering.

## Setting one up

1. Decide which assessments are interchangeable (for example, a main exam and its makeup).
2. Create a group and add them as members.
3. Mark **one** of them as the **primary**.
4. Save. The group now behaves as a single graded slot.

<Callout type="info">
The primary carries the grading weight, but a learner is never penalized for taking the makeup
instead — their actual score is the one that counts.
</Callout>

## How it affects grades

Because the slot contributes a single grade, it slots cleanly into your grade calculation. Set
how much that slot is worth using
[weighted grading](/docs/knowledgebase/progress-grading/weighted-grading).
