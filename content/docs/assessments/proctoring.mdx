Let us be clear about the limits first, because it saves you planning around something
that is not there.

<Callout type="warn">
**There is no webcam proctoring.** Edushade does not watch a learner through their
camera, lock their browser, record their screen, or detect another person in the room.
Nothing in the platform supervises a learner taking an exam at home.
</Callout>

What it does give you is a set of settings that, used together, make an unsupervised
assessment considerably harder to game — and make an invigilated one practical to run.

<WhoCan roles="Admin, Instructor" />

## What you can actually do

### Make two learners' papers different

| Setting | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Shuffle questions** | Every learner gets a different order. |
| **Shuffle answer options** | Options move within each question. |

Side-by-side copying stops being useful, because "C" is not the same answer twice.

### Limit the window

| Setting | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Time limit** | A learner has that many minutes, not an afternoon. |
| **Allow Pause** off | The clock does not stop. |
| **Availability window** | The quiz exists only between the dates you set. |

A tight window is the single most effective setting. An exam open for 90 minutes on one
evening is a very different thing from one open all week.

### Limit the attempts

**Maximum Attempts** of 1, with the programme's replacement policy set to **Replace**,
means one sitting and one mark. See
[Attempts and retakes](/docs/knowledgebase/assessments/attempts-and-retakes).

### Hold the results

Switch **Show each learner's score as soon as they submit** off and set **Release scores
on** to a time after the window closes. Nobody leaving early can tell the queue what
came up.

### Control who starts

**Password protected** gives you a password to hand out in the room — which is what
makes it useful for an invigilated sitting, and nearly useless for a remote one.

### Draw on a bigger pool

Keeping many questions in the
[Content Bank](/docs/knowledgebase/courses/content-bank) and building several versions
of a paper is slower to set up and much harder to defeat than any single setting.

## What you can see afterwards

Honesty is also something you check after the fact:

- **Participants** — who sat it and when.
- **Non-participants** — who did not.
- **Quiz analytics** — scores, time taken, and how each question performed. An answer
  pattern that is identical across three learners, or a 40-minute paper finished in
  four, shows up here.

See [Quiz analytics](/docs/knowledgebase/assessments/quiz-analytics).

## A workable recipe for a real exam

1. Shuffle questions and options.
2. Time limit on, **Allow Pause** off.
3. Maximum Attempts 1.
4. A narrow availability window.
5. Scores held, released after the window.
6. **Show answer after activity** off.
7. Check the analytics before you publish results.

That is as far as the platform goes. Beyond it, you are into invigilation — in a room,
or with a service outside Edushade.

## Common questions

<Accordions type="single">

<Accordion title="Can I stop learners opening another tab?">
No. Nothing in the platform watches or restricts what else is on their screen.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I require a camera during the exam?">
No. There is no camera requirement and no identity check during an assessment.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="How do I know who actually sat it?">
You know which account did. Identity beyond the account is not something the platform
can establish, which is why high-stakes exams are usually invigilated in person.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Is there anything that flags suspicious attempts?">
Not automatically. The analytics give you the evidence — time taken, scores, question
performance — and you read it.
</Accordion>
