title: "Question Reports"
description: "Problems learners flagged with a quiz's questions — and why one report usually means more than one learner."
---

Inside a quiz, under every question, a learner can report a problem. Those reports land
on the quiz's **Reports** screen.

They are worth reading the day they arrive, because a wrong answer key does not only
affect the learner who noticed.

<WhoCan roles="Admin, Instructor" />

## What learners can report

On a quiz question they are offered:

| Reason |
|---|
| The content is unclear |
| Something is wrong or out of date |
| An image or visual problem |
| A typing or language mistake |
| Something technical |
| Something else |

They can add their own description — *"the answer marked correct is the wrong one"*.

## Why a report on a question is urgent

A broken video annoys one learner. A wrong answer key gives a **wrong mark to everyone
who sat the quiz**, and nothing re-marks them when you fix the question.

So a question report needs two actions, not one:

1. **Fix the question**, so nobody else meets it.
2. **Correct the marks already given**, by overriding that question on each affected
   attempt and then recalculating the programme's grades.

[Grading quizzes](/docs/knowledgebase/assessments/grading-quizzes) walks through the
second one step by step.

## Confirming it before you act

Before changing an answer key on one learner's word, check the
[analytics](/docs/knowledgebase/assessments/quiz-analytics). A question that almost
everybody got "wrong" is the signature of a bad key. A question most people got right is
more likely to be one learner's misreading — which is still worth answering, just not
with a correction.

## Closing a report

Reports move through **New**, **Being looked at**, **Resolved** and **Dismissed**. When
you resolve one you can leave a note — *"fixed the answer key"* — which is what the
learner is shown.

Several learners reporting the same question produce several reports. Resolve them
together so everyone who took the trouble hears back.

## Common questions

<Accordions type="single">

<Accordion title="A learner is right that the key was wrong. What about the others?">
They have wrong marks, and fixing the question does not reach them. Find them on the
quiz's **Participants** list and override that question on each attempt, then run
**Calculate All Grades**.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Where do reports about lessons go?">
To the same place — the academy-wide
[Reported Problems](/docs/knowledgebase/courses/reported-problems) queue. The quiz's
**Reports** screen is the slice about that quiz's questions.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can learners report during an exam?">
Yes. It is the right way for them to flag a broken question rather than losing time on
it, which is a reason to tell them it is there in the instructions.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Nobody reports anything. Is it working?">
Probably. Most learners assume a broken question is their own misunderstanding. Mention
the link in your quiz instructions and the reports start arriving.
</Accordion>

</Accordions>
