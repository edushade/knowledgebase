Quizzes are graded in two ways: **automatically** for objective questions, and **by hand** for
written or creative answers. Most quizzes use a mix of both. This page explains how scoring
works and how you grade the parts that need a human.

## Auto-grading

Objective question types score themselves the instant a learner submits. This includes
multiple choice, true/false, dropdown, matching, ordering, fill-in-the-blank, numeric, and
more — see [Question types](/docs/knowledgebase/assessments/question-types).

The learner can get an **instant partial score** from these questions right away.

### Partial credit

Some auto-graded questions can award **part** of the points instead of all-or-nothing:

- **Multiple choice (multiple answers)** — credit for each correct option chosen.
- **Matching** — each pair can be scored on its own.
- **Fill-in-the-blank** — each blank is checked separately, and you can make each blank
  case-sensitive or not.
- **Numeric** — you set an **accepted tolerance**, so an answer close enough to the target
  still counts as correct.

## Scoring rules you control

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Points per question** | How much each question is worth. |
| **Negative marking** | Optionally subtract points for a wrong answer. |
| **Pass mark** | The percentage a learner must reach to pass. |

A learner's score is the total points earned, and pass/fail comes from the pass mark.

## Manual grading

Written and creative answers — short answer, essay, open ended, code, file upload, audio,
video, draw/sketch, comprehension, and case study — need a person to grade them.

1. Go to **Admin → Quizzes**, open the quiz, and find the attempts that need grading.
2. Open an attempt. Questions waiting on you are marked **pending**.
3. Read the learner's answer, then enter a **score** for that question.
4. Add **feedback** so the learner understands the mark.
5. Save. Once all pending questions are graded, the learner's **final score** updates.

<Callout type="info">
A quiz with any manually graded questions shows the learner an instant partial score for the
auto-graded part, then the final score after you finish grading.
</Callout>

## Overriding a score

Sometimes an auto-graded answer needs a human decision — for example, a valid answer the
system did not anticipate. You can **override** the score on any question and enter the mark
you want, along with feedback explaining why.

## What the learner sees

After grading is complete, the learner sees their result according to your display settings —
the score, pass/fail, and optionally the correct answers, explanations, and your feedback. See
[Quiz settings](/docs/knowledgebase/assessments/quiz-settings) and
[Taking quizzes](/docs/knowledgebase/assessments/taking-quizzes).

## How scores feed the final grade

When a quiz is used as a graded assessment inside a course, its score flows into the learner's
overall grade. You decide how much it counts using
[weighted grading](/docs/knowledgebase/progress-grading/weighted-grading).
