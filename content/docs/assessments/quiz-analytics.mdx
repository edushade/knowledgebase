A quiz's analytics answer two different questions. *How did the learners do?* is the
obvious one. *Is this quiz any good?* is the one worth asking.

<WhoCan roles="Admin, Instructor" />

## The figures

| Figure | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| **Attempts** | How many sittings, across everyone. |
| **Average score** | The middle of the distribution. |
| **Best score** | The top. |
| **Average time** | How long it actually takes — useful for setting a time limit next term. |
| **Completion rate** | The share of those who could sit it who did. |

Alongside them, three lists:

- **Participants** — who sat it, when, and the state of each attempt.
- **Non-participants** — who did not. This is the one you act on.
- **Leaderboard** — the ranking.

## Reading the quiz, not the learners

Per-question figures are where a quiz gets better:

| Pattern | What it usually means |
|---|---|
| Almost everyone got it **wrong** | A wrong answer key, or a question nobody was taught. Check the key first. |
| Almost everyone got it **right** | It is not telling you anything. Keep one or two of these as openers; drop the rest. |
| One option never chosen | A wrong answer that is obviously wrong. Replace it and the question gets harder honestly. |
| The quiz finished far faster than its time limit | The limit is not doing anything. |

<Callout type="info">
**Check the per-question figures before you release results.** A bad answer key is much
cheaper to fix before anyone has seen a mark than afterwards.
</Callout>

## Comparing two quizzes

Where a programme has at least two quizzes, you can compare learners' scores between
them — did the class improve between the mock and the final, and who went backwards. It
needs two quizzes to work; with one, there is nothing to compare.

## Non-participants is the useful list

Average score tells you about the people who turned up. **Non-participants** tells you
about the ones who did not, and that is usually the bigger number and the bigger
problem.

It can be filtered by programme, so "who in Batch 12 has not sat the mid-term" is a
list you can act on — see [Campaigns](/docs/knowledgebase/communication/campaigns) for
sending them something.

| Question | Screen |

## Common questions

<Accordions type="single">

<Accordion title="The average score dropped sharply this term.">
Check whether the paper changed, and check the per-question figures. A single bad
question moves an average more than a weaker cohort does.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="The completion rate looks wrong.">
It counts the learners who could sit it. Someone without access to that content — through
partial access or an eligibility rule — is not counted against it.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I export the attempts?">
Yes, from the participants list.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Why does a learner appear twice?">
They have more than one attempt. Which one counts towards their grade follows the
programme's replacement policy, not the order in this list.
</Accordion>

</Accordions>
