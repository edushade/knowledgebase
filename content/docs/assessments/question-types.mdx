A question's type decides how a learner answers it and whether it can be marked without
a person. In a quiz, **six types are available, and all six mark themselves** — a quiz
is scored the moment it is submitted.

<WhoCan roles="Admin, Instructor" />

## The six

**Add Question** in the quiz builder opens the picker, grouped by what the question
asks of the learner.

### Objective — auto-gradable

| Type | The learner… | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| **Multiple Choice (Single)** | Picks one answer. | Checking a single fact or judgement. |
| **Multiple Choice (Multiple)** | Picks several. | "Which three of these apply?" |
| **True or False** | Chooses between two statements. | Quick checks; easy to guess, so use several. |
| **Dropdown Selection** | Chooses from a dropdown menu. | Long option lists that would crowd the page. |
| **Fill in the Blank** | Types the missing word. | Terminology and recall, where recognising is too easy. |

### Composite — interactive

| Type | The learner… | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| **Matching** | Pairs items from two columns. | Terms to definitions, causes to effects, steps to their order. |

Every one of these is scored on submission, so a quiz made of them gives the learner a
result straight away and gives you a mark with no queue behind it.

## When you need written work

A quiz cannot ask for an essay, a paragraph or a free written answer. That is what
[assignments](/docs/knowledgebase/assessments/assignments) are for: a learner writes or
uploads, you read it and give it a mark, and it counts towards their grade like any
other assessment.

<Callout type="info">
**The split is deliberate, and it is worth using.** A quiz gives an instant, objective
result at scale. An assignment gives you something to read. Most courses want some of
each, and the [grading scheme](/docs/knowledgebase/progress-grading/course-grading-setup)
is where you decide how much each is worth.
</Callout>

If you need to test reasoning without the marking, a well-built Multiple Choice
(Multiple) comes closer than people expect — "which of these would you do, and which
would you not" forces the same judgement.

If you need ordering, Matching does the job: steps on one side, positions on the other.

## What every question takes

| Field | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Question Title** | The question itself. Required. |
| **Score** | What it is worth, from 0.1 to 100. |
| **Required** | Whether the learner may leave it blank. Nothing to do with marks. |
| **Explanation** | Shown after the answer, where you have allowed that. |
| **Shuffle Options** | Scramble this question's options. |
| **Time limit** | A limit for this one question, on top of the quiz's. |
| **Image / audio / video / attachments** | Media the question is about. |

Multiple choice, true/false and dropdown questions need their options; matching needs
its pairs, and can carry **distractors** — extra right-hand items that match nothing,
which stop a learner solving the last pair by elimination.

## Choosing well

- **Multiple choice is only as good as its wrong answers.** Three obviously silly
  options make a question everyone gets right, and it tells you nothing.
- **True or False is a coin toss** on its own. Use it in groups of several.
- **Fill in the Blank punishes spelling.** Leave **Case sensitive** off, accept the
  variants you can think of, and check
  [question reports](/docs/knowledgebase/assessments/question-reports) afterwards for the
  one everybody got "wrong".
- **Write the explanation while you write the question.** It is the cheapest teaching in
  the course, and you will not come back to add it later.

## Where questions live

A question can be written inside a quiz, or kept in the
[Content Bank](/docs/knowledgebase/courses/content-bank) and reused. The Content Bank
offers the same six types.

A banked question pulled into a quiz becomes that quiz's own copy — later edits to the
bank do not reach it.

## Common questions

<Accordions type="single">

<Accordion title="Can I ask an essay question in a quiz?">
No. Use an [assignment](/docs/knowledgebase/assessments/assignments) — it is built for
written work, it carries a mark, and that mark counts towards the grade the same way a
quiz does.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What does Required mean?">
That the learner cannot submit while it is blank. It does not affect marks, and it is
not the same as **Require All Questions**, which is a quiz-wide setting.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I change a score after a learner has answered?">
Yes. Any score can be overridden from the grading screen — useful when an answer key
turns out to be wrong. See
[Fixing a wrong answer key](/docs/knowledgebase/how-to/fix-a-wrong-answer-key).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Is Fill in the Blank case sensitive?">
There is a **Case sensitive** setting on the question. Leave it off unless the case is
genuinely part of the answer, or learners will fail on a capital letter.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What are distractors in a Matching question?">
Extra items on the right that pair with nothing. Without them a learner who knows three
of four pairs gets the fourth free.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can a question be worth negative marks?">
A question can carry a negative score for a wrong answer, set beside its score. Use it
carefully — it punishes guessing, and it punishes learners who are unsure but right.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="I have older questions of a type that is not in the picker.">
Questions made before a type was withdrawn still work — learners can answer them and
they still count. You just cannot add another one of that type. If you are rebuilding
the quiz, replace it with one of the six.
</Accordion>

</Accordions>
