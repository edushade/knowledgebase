---
title: "How to Chase Money You Are Owed"
description: "Four lists on the billing overview, in the order that recovers the most."
---

Every academy has money sitting in four places. Twenty minutes a week on these four
lists is worth more than any report.

<WhoCan roles="Admin" />

## 1. Offline payments to verify

**Somebody has paid and cannot get in.** This is the unhappiest customer you have, and
the only one on this page whose problem is entirely yours.

<Path>Billing > Transactions > Pending Verification</Path>.

Confirm or turn down each one. Clear it daily. See
[Check a direct payment](/docs/knowledgebase/how-to/check-a-direct-payment).

## 2. Instalments overdue

Grouped by how late, and the grouping is the point:

| Group | What to send |
|---|---|
| **Due in the next 7 days** | A reminder. Most people simply forget. |
| **1–7 days late** | A friendly nudge, with how to pay. |
| **8–30 days late** | A direct message. Mention that access pauses after the grace period. |
| **Over 30 days late** | A conversation, not a message. |
| **Past grace — access paused** | They cannot get in. Tell them what to pay and that access returns at once. |

The last group is important: their enrolment looks live and nothing opens. A learner
who does not know why assumes the platform is broken.

[Installments](/docs/knowledgebase/selling-payments/installments)

## 3. Failed payments and abandoned checkouts

**Failed payments** — they tried and it did not go through. Some have already solved it;
some gave up.

**Checkouts not completed** — they got to payment and stopped.

Both are people who decided to buy. One message, with a direct link back, recovers a
useful share.

## 4. Lapsed and expiring access

**Lapsed subscriptions** and **renewals overdue**: access that has run out.

The better move is earlier. Build an audience of learners whose access ends in the next
seven days and send a reminder before it lapses — recovery after is far harder than
prevention before.

[Audiences](/docs/knowledgebase/communication/audiences) ·
[Subscriptions](/docs/knowledgebase/selling-payments/subscriptions)

## Making it a routine

<Steps>

<Step>

### Set up the audiences once

Access ending in 7 days · instalment due in 7 days · instalment 1–7 days late ·
checkout not completed.

</Step>

<Step>

### Write the messages once

Four templates, each saying what is owed, how to pay, and what happens if they do not.

[Message templates](/docs/knowledgebase/communication/message-templates)

</Step>

<Step>

### Work the lists weekly

Verification first, then overdue instalments by group, then the near-misses.

</Step>

<Step>

### Check it is landing

[Delivery and analytics](/docs/knowledgebase/communication/delivery-and-analytics).
A reminder that did not arrive is not a reminder.

</Step>

</Steps>

## Before chasing anybody

Check the money is not already in. A payment taken in cash and never recorded looks
exactly like an unpaid instalment — and chasing somebody who has paid costs more
goodwill than the instalment is worth.
