---
title: "How to Set Up Grading for a Programme"
description: "Scheme, weights, rounding and the two settings that decide most results — in the order that avoids recalculating everyone."
---

Grading is five decisions. Make them before learners have marks, and nothing needs
redoing.

<WhoCan roles="Admin" />

**Before you start** — the programme should have its assessments in place, because step
three weights them.

<Steps>

<Step>

### Build the scale, once

<Path>Grading > Grading Schemes</Path>, then create one. Bands from the top down, each with its
percentage range, letter, grade point and whether it is a **passing grade**.

Two rules:

- **No gaps.** A result between two bands gets no grade at all.
- **Get the pass line right.** It decides passing, certificates and progression, all
  three.

Choose **GPA** if you report grade points, **Percentage** if you do not.

[Grading schemes](/docs/knowledgebase/progress-grading/grading-schemes)

</Step>

<Step>

### Point the programme at it

On the programme's **Grading** tab, choose the scheme. **Calculation Method** is
**Weighted** and cannot be changed.

A batch can have its own setup; without one it follows its course.

[Course grading setup](/docs/knowledgebase/progress-grading/course-grading-setup)

</Step>

<Step>

### Set the weights

In the weights editor, give each assessment a weight. **They must total exactly 100%** —
save is refused until they do.

Switch **Auto-distribute** on, type only the weights you care about — those get pinned —
and the rest share what is left. **Distribute equally** starts again from scratch.

Set no weights at all and the programme falls back to a plain average.

[Weighted grading](/docs/knowledgebase/progress-grading/weighted-grading)

</Step>

<Step>

### Settle rounding and precision

**Decimal Places** (0–4, usually 2) and **Rounding**.

A grade is matched to its band **after** rounding, so this moves the pass line. At a
pass mark of 50, a learner on 49.6 passes under "round to nearest" and fails under
"always down".

Leave Rounding unset and the programme follows what sits above it.

[Rounding and precision](/docs/knowledgebase/progress-grading/rounding-and-precision)

</Step>

<Step>

### Decide the two settings that matter most

**Treat Ungraded Items as Zero.** Off (the default), unmarked work is left out and its
weight is shared among what has been marked — so a learner who has sat one assessment is
marked on that one alone. On, unmarked work counts as zero.

Off while the course runs. On at the end, if skipping an assessment should cost
something — and tell learners before you switch it.

**Allow Grade Replacement**, and its policy: **Replace**, **Average** or **Best**. This
decides which attempt counts when somebody sits an assessment twice.

[Course grading setup](/docs/knowledgebase/progress-grading/course-grading-setup) ·
[Retakes and replacement](/docs/knowledgebase/progress-grading/retakes-and-replacement)

</Step>

<Step>

### Check it on one learner

Open the programme's **Learner Grades**, pick somebody with marks, and read their
breakdown. Do the subject figures add up to the result beside them? Does the grade
letter match the band you meant?

Five minutes here saves a term of wrong results.

</Step>

</Steps>

## After any change

Run **Calculate All Grades** on the programme. Results are stored as they are
calculated — without it, some learners carry results from the old rules and some from
the new.

## If something goes wrong

**Save is refused on the weights.** They do not total 100%. With bonus items in play,
only the items marked **Full** count towards it.

**A result has no grade letter.** No scheme, or a gap between two bands.

**Everyone's result looks too high.** **Treat Ungraded Items as Zero** is off and most
work is unmarked. That is correct behaviour mid-course.

**A batch grades differently from its course.** It has its own setup, or it has none and
is following the course's.
