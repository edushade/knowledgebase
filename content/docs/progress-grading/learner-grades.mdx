Every programme has a **Learner Grades** screen: everyone enrolled, with their result.
Open a learner and you get their breakdown — subjects, the assessments inside them, and
each semester.

This is where you go when somebody says "my mark is wrong".

<WhoCan roles="Admin, Instructor" />

## Working down to the wrong number

A result is built in four stages, so a wrong result has four possible causes. Check them
in this order — it is almost always the lowest one:

| Level | Check |
|---|---|
| **1. The assessment** | Did it get marked? Does the mark match what the learner actually scored? |
| **2. Which attempt** | If they sat it twice, is the right attempt counting? See [Retakes and replacement](/docs/knowledgebase/progress-grading/retakes-and-replacement). |
| **3. The subject** | Are the weights right, and is anything unmarked being left out? |
| **4. The programme** | Is the right scheme, rounding and result policy in use? |

Two settings explain most surprises:

- **Treat Ungraded Items as Zero** — off, a learner is marked only on what has been
  marked, so one good quiz can read as a near-perfect course.
- **Rounding** — a result is matched to its band after rounding, so a learner on 49.6
  may pass or fail depending on the direction.

## Correcting a mark

You can edit a mark directly. The grade form takes the learner, the item, the score and
the status, and it keeps internal comments that the learner never sees — a note saying
why you changed it, for the next person who asks.

Use it for genuine corrections: a question marked wrong by a bad answer key, work
submitted on paper, an agreed adjustment. For a learner who should sit the assessment
again, give them another attempt instead of editing the number — see
[Attempts and retakes](/docs/knowledgebase/assessments/attempts-and-retakes).

<Callout type="warn">
Editing one assessment does not automatically redo every figure above it. After a
correction that matters, run **Calculate All Grades** on the programme so the subject,
semester and final results all agree.
</Callout>

## Recalculating everyone

**Calculate All Grades**, on the programme's learner grades screen, runs the current
rules over every learner. It works in the background and can be cancelled while it is
running.

Run it after you change weights, the grading scheme, the rounding, the result policy, or
**Treat Ungraded Items as Zero**. Without it, some learners carry results from the old
rules and some from the new, and the two will not agree.

## Per-learner, across programmes

From a person's own record you also get their grades across everything they are in, split
into quizzes and assignments. That is the view for "how is this learner doing overall?"
rather than "how did this course go?".

## Common questions

<Accordions type="single">

<Accordion title="A learner has a result for a quiz they never sat.">
Check for an alternative group — if the quiz is interchangeable with another, the slot
shows the member they actually took. See
[Alternative assessments](/docs/knowledgebase/assessments/alternative-assessments).
Otherwise check for a transfer: marks can follow a learner from another batch.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Two learners with identical marks have different results.">
Look for partial access or a transfer. A learner marked on one semester is marked out of
that semester. See [Partial access](/docs/knowledgebase/enrollment-access/partial-access).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="I edited a mark and the overall result did not change.">
Run **Calculate All Grades**. Results above the assessment level are stored as they are
calculated, not worked out afresh each time they are read.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="An assignment is submitted but has no mark.">
It is waiting for someone to mark it. The programme's assignment submissions screen is
the queue. See [Assignment submissions](/docs/knowledgebase/assessments/assignment-submissions).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can an instructor edit grades?">
If their role allows it. Being named on a programme and being allowed to change marks
are separate permissions. See
[People and roles](/docs/knowledgebase/account-people/people-and-roles).
</Accordion>

</Accordions>
