---
title: "Completion Rules"
description: "What a learner has to do for a lesson, file, quiz or assignment to count as finished — and how to change it for one item or one person."
---

Progress is the share of a course a learner has finished. A completion rule decides what
"finished" means for each piece: opened it, watched most of it, sat the quiz, or passed
it.

Every piece of content already has a sensible rule. You only set one when the default
is wrong for you.

<WhoCan roles="Admin, Instructor" />

## The rules

### For lessons and files

| Rule | What the learner must do |
|---|---|
| **Default (built-in)** | Whatever the platform normally does for this kind of content. |
| **Viewed / Opened** | Just open it. Done immediately. |
| **Reach a percentage** | Get to a set point — watched, read, or pages turned. You give the percentage. |
| **Spend minimum time** | Stay with it for a number of minutes. You give the minutes. |

**Spend minimum time** is available for video and audio. A reading — text or PDF — does
not offer it; use **Reach a percentage** for those.

### For quizzes and assignments

| Rule | What the learner must do |
|---|---|
| **Default (built-in)** | The normal behaviour for an assessment. |
| **Attempt only** | Sit it or submit it. Passing is not required. |
| **Attempted with a Pass** | Score at or above the pass mark. A fail does not count. |

<Callout type="warn">
**Attempted with a Pass can lock a learner out.** If a quiz has to be passed to count as
done, and something later is waiting for it, a learner who keeps failing goes nowhere.
Check how many attempts the quiz allows before choosing it. See
[Prerequisites](/docs/knowledgebase/enrollment-access/prerequisites).
</Callout>

## Where a rule can be set

Rules are set at four levels. The most specific one that applies wins:

1. **Across your whole academy** — "every video counts when 90% watched".
2. **For one programme** — the same idea, for this course only.
3. **For one item** — from the item's **⋯** menu in the course builder, **Completion
   Rule**.
4. **For particular learners** — an override on that item, for named people only.

A per-learner override is for the genuine exception: a learner with a disability who
cannot meet a time rule, a makeup arrangement. Pick the item, choose **Edit override**,
and name the learners. The screen stops you giving one person two overrides on the same
item, and tells you which of your selection already has one.

## What progress counts

Only **published** content counts. Unpublished items are not in the course as far as
progress is concerned — which is why unpublishing something everybody has finished moves
everyone's percentage.

<Callout type="info">
A **file** attached to a lesson is not an item and never counts. A **file item** in the
course outline does. See
[Files and resources](/docs/knowledgebase/courses/files-and-resources).
</Callout>

## Choosing well

- **A video you want actually watched** → Reach a percentage, around 90%. Not 100%:
  learners stop at the credits and never get the tick.
- **A reference PDF** → Viewed / Opened. It is there to be consulted, not read end to
  end.
- **A practice quiz** → Attempt only. The point is trying.
- **A gate before the next module** → Attempted with a Pass, with enough attempts
  allowed that a learner can actually get through.

## Common questions

<Accordions type="single">

<Accordion title="A learner watched the whole video but it is not ticked.">
Check the lesson's **Duration**. Progress is measured against the duration you typed, so
if it is longer than the real video the learner can never reach the percentage. See
[Lesson types](/docs/knowledgebase/courses/lesson-types).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Everyone's progress dropped after I edited the course.">
You probably unpublished or deleted an item people had finished. Progress is a share of
the published content, so removing a finished item changes the share for everyone.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="An embedded video is not counting as watched.">
Check what the embed says it contains. An embed set to **Other** gets no assumption made
about it; set it to **Video** and it inherits the video rule. See
[Lesson types](/docs/knowledgebase/courses/lesson-types).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can a completion rule be different in two courses for the same quiz?">
Yes. The rule is attached to the item where it sits, so a shared quiz can count on
attempt in one course and only on a pass in another.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="How do I remove an override?">
Open the item's completion rule and choose **Remove override** for that learner. They go
back to following whatever rule applies to everyone else.
</Accordion>

</Accordions>
