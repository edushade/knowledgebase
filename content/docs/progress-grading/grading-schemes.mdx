A grading scheme is your scale: 90–100% is an **A** worth **4.0**, and an A is a pass.
You build it once in <Path>Grading > Grading Schemes</Path> and every programme that uses it
reports grades the same way.

A scheme decides *what a number is called*. It does not decide *how the number is
worked out* — that is the programme's
[grading setup](/docs/knowledgebase/progress-grading/course-grading-setup).

## What a scheme holds

| Field | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Name** | What you call it — "US Standard 4.0 GPA", "Diploma marksheet". |
| **Code** | A short unique identifier, such as `US_4.0`. |
| **Scale Type** | **GPA** or **Percentage**. See below — this is the important one. |
| **Max Scale Value** | The top of the scale, e.g. 4.0. Asked for only when the scale type is GPA. |
| **Description** | A note for your team. |
| **Set as Default** | New programmes use this scheme unless you choose another. |
| **Active** | Switch a scheme off without deleting it. |

## GPA or Percentage

This is the choice that changes what learners read on their results.

| | **GPA** | **Percentage** |
|---|---|---|
| What a result shows | The percentage, its band, **and a grade point** | The percentage and its band |
| What a transcript carries | A GPA and a CGPA | Percentages only |
| Max Scale Value | Asked for | Not asked for |

<Callout type="info">
**Switching to Percentage does not delete your grade points.** They stay on the bands;
Percentage simply decides not to show them *to learners*. Switch back to GPA and they
are there again.
</Callout>

Your own staff screens are different: the **Grade Point** column appears on a learner's
semester results wherever points are actually recorded, whichever scale type you chose.
A percentage scale decides how a learner's result is presented — it does not hide a
figure from the people whose job is to read what was stored.

## The bands

A scheme is a list of bands, from the top down. Each one takes:

| Field | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Grade Letter** | The label — A+, B, Pass. Required. |
| **Grade Point** | What the band is worth, e.g. 4.0. Required. |
| **Min Percentage** / **Max Percentage** | The range this band covers. Required. |
| **Is Passing Grade** | Whether a learner in this band has passed. |
| **Qualitative Label** | A longer description — "Outstanding", "First Class". |
| **Description** | A note for your team. |
| **Display Order** | Where the band sits in the list. |

A workable scheme:

| Percentage | Grade | Point | Passing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 90–100% | A | 4.0 | Yes |
| 80–89% | B | 3.5 | Yes |
| 70–79% | C | 3.0 | Yes |
| 60–69% | D | 2.0 | Yes |
| 0–59% | F | 0.0 | **No** |

<Callout type="warn">
**Leave no gap between bands.** If one band ends at 89 and the next begins at 90, a
result of 89.5 belongs to neither and the learner gets no grade at all. Make them meet:
89 and 90 with nothing between, or 89.99 and 90.
</Callout>

## Is Passing Grade is doing more than it looks

This single switch decides a great deal elsewhere:

- Whether a learner has **passed the programme**.
- Whether they are eligible for a **certificate** — see
  [Certificate requirements](/docs/knowledgebase/certificates/certificate-requirements).
- Whether they **move up a semester** or repeat — see
  [Progression](/docs/knowledgebase/enrollment-access/progression).

Get the pass line wrong and three other things quietly go wrong with it.

## Rounding moves the pass line

A result is matched to its band **after** it has been rounded. With the pass line at 50
and rounding set to nearest, a learner on 49.6 is rounded to 50 and passes. With
rounding set to always down, they do not.

That is not a detail — it is the pass line. See
[Rounding and precision](/docs/knowledgebase/progress-grading/rounding-and-precision).

## Managing your schemes

From <Path>Grading > Grading Schemes</Path> you can create a scheme, edit one, mark one as the
default, and deactivate one you have stopped using.

Most academies need one scheme. Make a second when a programme genuinely marks
differently — a diploma on a 4.0 GPA and a short course reported as a plain percentage.

## Common questions

<Accordions type="single">

<Accordion title="A learner's result shows no grade letter.">
Almost always a gap between two bands. Check that every percentage from 0 to 100 falls
inside exactly one band, with no space left between them.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I change a scheme that programmes are already using?">
Yes, and the change applies to results calculated from then on. Results already
recorded keep the grade they were given until they are recalculated. If you move the
pass line mid-course, recalculate so everyone is judged by the same scale — see
[Course grading setup](/docs/knowledgebase/progress-grading/course-grading-setup).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Where did Letter and Points go?">
Only **GPA** and **Percentage** are offered. Letter and Points are not available.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Do I need a scheme at all?">
To report a grade, yes. Without one a programme can still record marks, but there is no
scale to turn them into a grade, a pass, or a certificate.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can two programmes use different schemes?">
Yes. The scheme is chosen per programme in its grading setup. One scheme can be the
default for new programmes while a particular diploma uses another.
</Accordion>

</Accordions>
