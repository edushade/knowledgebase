---
title: "Reviews and Ratings"
description: "Star ratings and written reviews — switching them on, approving them, and what a rating is connected to."
---

A review is a learner's verdict on a course: a star rating and, usually, something
written. It appears on the course page, where the next buyer reads it.

Each programme has a **Reviews** tab.

<WhoCan roles="Admin, Instructor" />

## Switching it on

**Enable Rating** on the course's settings step — "allow students to rate the course".
Off, there are no reviews on that programme at all.

## Approving them

Reviews move through **Pending**, **Approved** and **Rejected**. A review is not public
until it is approved.

The **Reviews** tab shows each one with its **Reviewer Name**, **Rating**, **Review
Date** and the text, and lets you approve or reject it.

<Callout type="warn">
**Rejecting criticism is a trap.** A course page with nothing but five stars reads as
unreviewed, not as excellent. Reject what is abusive or irrelevant; approve the honest
three-star review and answer it.
</Callout>

## A review can be a certificate requirement

A [certificate type](/docs/knowledgebase/certificates/certificate-types) can require an
**approved review**. The learner leaves a review, you approve it, and the certificate
follows.

Two things to know:

- **It needs your approval**, so an unapproved queue holds up certificates.
- **If a programme has rating switched off**, that requirement is dropped rather than
  counted as impossible — otherwise a certificate requiring ALL of its conditions could
  never be earned.

## Where ratings show up

| Place | What it shows |
|---|---|
| The course page | The rating and the reviews. |
| Your catalogue | The average rating. |
| [Programme analytics](/docs/knowledgebase/analytics/programmes) | **Completion and Rating Trend**, together. |

Reading rating beside completion is the honest version. A course rated highly by the few
people who finish it is not a good course.

## Getting reviews at all

Most learners never review anything. What works:

1. **Ask at the moment they finish**, not weeks later.
2. **Make it a certificate requirement**, where that suits your academy.
3. **Answer the ones you get**, publicly. Learners review a course where reviews are
   read.

## Common questions

<Accordions type="single">

<Accordion title="A learner's review is not showing.">
It is pending approval. Nothing is public until approved.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can a learner review a course they did not take?">
Reviews come from enrolled learners.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I reply to a review?">
There is no reply field on a review. Answer it in your course discussion or directly —
and if the criticism is fair, fix the course.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="A learner is waiting for a certificate that needs a review.">
Check your pending reviews. An unapproved review holds up their certificate.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I delete a review?">
Reject it. It stops being public.
</Accordion>

</Accordions>
