---
title: "Reactions"
description: "The six reactions learners can leave, and what they are actually good for."
---

A reaction is a one-tap response. It costs a learner nothing, which is exactly why they
use it when they would not write a reply.

<WhoCan roles="Learner, Instructor, Admin" />

## The six

| Reaction | Usually means |
|---|---|
| **Like** | Agreed, or useful. |
| **Love** | Strongly liked. |
| **Care** | Sympathy. |
| **Haha** | Amusement. |
| **Sad** | Sympathy, or disappointment. |
| **Angry** | Frustration. |

A person has one reaction per item — reacting again changes it, and reacting with the
same one removes it.

## What they are good for

**Reading a quiet room.** A lesson with thirty "like" reactions and no comments is
working. One with three "sad" reactions and no comments is not, and nobody was going to
tell you.

**Letting learners endorse an answer.** In a discussion, reactions point at the reply
that actually helped — which is more useful than it sounds when a thread is long.

**Costing nothing.** Most learners will never write a comment. Almost all of them will
tap once.

## What they are not

Reactions are not a measurement. There is no reaction report, and they do not feed
analytics. They are a signal to read, not a number to track.

For actual sentiment, use
[reviews and ratings](/docs/knowledgebase/community/reviews-and-ratings); for whether
the course is working, use
[progress](/docs/knowledgebase/analytics/progress).

## Common questions

<Accordions type="single">

<Accordion title="Can I turn reactions off?">
They belong to the discussion and comment areas, which are enabled per programme with
**Enable Discussion**.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I add our own reaction?">
No. Those six are what exists.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I see who reacted?">
Reactions are shown on the item. There is no separate report.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Do reactions notify anyone?">
Not as a notification type. Replies and mentions do.
</Accordion>

</Accordions>
