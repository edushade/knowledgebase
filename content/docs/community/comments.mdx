A comment belongs to a specific thing: a lesson, a discussion, a post. It is the
smallest unit of conversation on your platform, and the one learners use most.

<WhoCan roles="Admin, Instructor, Learner" />

## What people can do

| Action | Who |
|---|---|
| **Post** a comment | Anyone who can see the content |
| **Reply** to one | The same |
| **Edit** their own | The author |
| **Delete** | The author, and moderators |

Deleting asks first — *"are you sure? This action cannot be undone."*

## Where comments appear

Under lessons, inside discussions, and on blog posts where you allow it. A comment under
a lesson is attached to that lesson, which is what makes it useful: the question is
beside the thing it is about.

## Moderating

Keep it light. Remove abuse and answers to assessments; leave the rest, including the
clumsy questions — they are what other learners search for.

Deletion is permanent, so for a comment that is merely unhelpful, a reply is usually
better than a removal.

## Keeping up with them

Learners are notified of **Discussion Reply Received** and **Mentioned in Discussion**,
which is what brings a conversation back to life. See
[Notification settings](/docs/knowledgebase/communication/notification-preferences).

## Common questions

<Accordions type="single">

<Accordion title="A learner cannot comment.">
Comments live on content they can see, and discussion has to be enabled for the
programme.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I switch comments off for one lesson?">
Discussion is enabled per programme, not per lesson.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can learners edit a comment after replies?">
An author can edit their own comment. An edit after a reply can leave the thread
confusing, which is a reason to reply rather than rewrite.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Is there a report button on comments?">
Learners can report a problem with the **content** — see
[Reported problems](/docs/knowledgebase/courses/reported-problems). For a comment,
moderation is yours.
</Accordion>

</Accordions>
