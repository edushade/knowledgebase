A discussion is a conversation attached to something you teach. Learners ask,
instructors answer, and the next learner with the same question finds the answer already
there.

<Path>Engage > Discussion</Path>, and on each programme's **Discussions** tab.

<WhoCan roles="Admin, Instructor, Learner" />

## What a discussion can be attached to

Five things, and the choice decides where learners meet it:

| Attached to | Where it appears |
|---|---|
| **Course** | Beside the course content. |
| **Learning Path** | Across the whole path, not one course in it. |
| **Cohort** | One batch only — the others cannot see it. |
| **Quiz** | On that quiz. Useful after results, less so before. |
| **Event** | Around a live session: questions before, follow-up after. |

**Cohort** is the one most academies under-use. A discussion on the course is shared by
every batch that ever runs it; a discussion on the batch belongs to those learners and
that term.

## Switching it on

**Enable Discussion** on the course's settings step — "allow students to discuss course
content". Off, there is no discussion area at all.

Each discussion is also **Public** or **Private**, which decides who can see it. The
list can be filtered by both **Entity Type** and **Privacy**, which is how you find one
again once you have more than a handful.

## Where learners meet it

Beside the content it belongs to. A learner stuck on lesson four asks there, and their
question sits where the next person will look.

## Keeping it alive

A discussion area with no replies is worse than none — it advertises that nobody is
listening.

Three things that work:

1. **Answer within a day, every day, for the first month.** A forum where questions get
   answered becomes a forum where questions get asked.
2. **Seed it.** Post the three questions every cohort asks, with their answers, before
   the batch starts.
3. **Let learners answer each other.** Thank the ones who do, publicly. The best
   discussion areas are mostly learners.

Notifications help: **Discussion Reply Received**, **Mentioned in Discussion** and
**Instructor Replied to Discussion** bring people back. See
[Notification settings](/docs/knowledgebase/communication/notification-preferences).

## Moderating

Comments can be edited and deleted — deletion cannot be undone and asks first. Keep a
light hand: a discussion that is heavily moderated stops being used.

What actually needs removing is rare — abuse, and answers to an assessment. The second
is worth saying out loud in your course instructions before it happens.

## Common questions

<Accordions type="single">

<Accordion title="Learners cannot find the discussion.">
**Enable Discussion** is off for that programme, or the discussion is private.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can instructors moderate their own courses only?">
What they can do comes from their role, and a role can be limited to the programmes they
are assigned to. See [People and roles](/docs/knowledgebase/account-people/people-and-roles).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Nobody is posting.">
Almost always because nobody answered the first few. Seed it, and answer quickly for the
first month.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Someone posted the answers to a quiz.">
Delete the comment, and say in your course instructions that it is not allowed. The
second part prevents the next one.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Is there private messaging between learners?">
No. Learner-to-learner messaging is not switched on. Discussions and comments are how
learners talk here.
</Accordion>

</Accordions>
