Search engines read your pages the way a visitor cannot: titles, descriptions,
structure. Getting those right is most of what you can do.

<WhoCan roles="Admin" />

## The switch that matters most

**Allow search engines to index this site.** Off, nothing on this page helps — your site
asks search engines to stay away.

<Callout type="error">
Check this first. A site switched off during building and never switched on is the
single most common reason an academy is invisible, and it can sit unnoticed for months.
</Callout>

## Per-page metadata

Every page has **Meta Title**, **Meta Description** and **Meta Keywords**, under its
**Metadata** tab.

| Field | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Meta Title** | The headline in search results. |
| **Meta Description** | The two lines underneath. Not a ranking factor, but it decides whether anybody clicks. |
| **Meta Keywords** | Largely ignored by search engines now. |

**Meta Title Template** sets the pattern for every page at once — for example
`{page_title} | {site_name}`.

Write the description as a promise to the person reading it: what they will get, in one
sentence, in their words.

## Site-wide settings

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Enable XML Sitemap** | Tells search engines what exists. Leave it on. |
| **Enable Structured Breadcrumbs** | Makes your site structure legible in results. |
| **Social Share Image** | The picture shown when a link is shared. |
| **Twitter Handle** | Attribution when your pages are shared. |
| **Enable Social Sharing** | Share buttons on your content. |

## Addresses

A page's **slug** is its address. Two rules:

1. **Make it readable.** `/courses/spoken-english` beats `/p/12345`.
2. **Do not change it once it is live.** If you must, set a redirect — see
   [Versions and redirects](/docs/knowledgebase/website/versions-and-redirects).

**URL Structure** also decides how languages appear in addresses — a subdirectory,
a subdomain, or a query parameter. See
[Languages](/docs/knowledgebase/website/languages).

## What actually brings an academy traffic

In order of return:

1. **Course pages that answer the question somebody typed.** "IELTS preparation course
   in Dhaka" is a page, not a keyword.
2. **A [blog](/docs/knowledgebase/website/blog) answering real questions.** The ones your
   learners ask before they buy.
3. **Titles and descriptions written for people**, not stuffed with terms.
4. **A site that is fast on a phone** — leave **Lazy Load Images** and **Minify CSS/JS**
   on.

## Common questions

<Accordions type="single">

<Accordion title="We are not in Google at all.">
Check indexing is allowed, the sitemap is on, and the site state is **Live**. Then
submit your sitemap to Google's own tools and wait — indexing is not instant.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Our page title shows wrongly in results.">
Search engines sometimes write their own. Make sure the meta title describes the page
honestly, and it is usually used.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="We changed a page's address and lost traffic.">
Set a redirect from the old address to the new one. See
[Versions and redirects](/docs/knowledgebase/website/versions-and-redirects).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Does the knowledge base of our courses get indexed?">
Your public pages are indexed; content behind enrolment is not. That is correct —
nobody should find your paid lessons in a search.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Should I fill in meta keywords?">
They do very little now. Spend the time on the title and description.
</Accordion>

</Accordions>
