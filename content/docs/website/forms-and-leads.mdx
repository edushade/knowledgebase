A form is how somebody who is not ready to buy tells you they exist. A contact form, an
enquiry, a scholarship application, a waiting list.

<Path>Configure > Website</Path>, under forms.

<WhoCan roles="Admin" />

## Building one

### Form information

| Field | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Title** | What it is called. |
| **Description** | Shown above the fields. |
| **Slug** | Its name in addresses — `contact-form`. |
| **Status** | Active or inactive. |

### The fields

Add fields in the **Form Builder**. Each takes a **Label**, a **Placeholder**, a field
name, and whether it is required. The types available include **Phone**, **Select**,
**Radio**, **Checkbox** and **File Upload**, alongside the ordinary text ones.

A field can also validate what is typed, which is how you stop a phone number field
collecting "call me".

<Callout type="info">
**Ask for less than you want.** Every field loses people. Name and phone is usually
enough to start a conversation; everything else can be asked once they reply.
</Callout>

## What happens on submit

### The person who submitted

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Success Message** | What they read immediately. |
| Redirect | Send them to a page instead — `/thank-you`. |
| **A reply for this form** | An automatic reply, written for this form. |
| **The site's reply** | Your site-wide default reply. |
| **No reply for this form** | Nothing is sent. |

Send a reply. Somebody who fills in a form and hears nothing assumes it did not work,
and half of them will not try again.

### Your team

**Admin notifications** decide who is told. Choose **people on your team** or whole
**roles**, and set the **Subject** — for example `New submission: {form_name}`.

<Callout type="warn">
**Name somebody specific.** A form whose notifications go to a shared address nobody
owns is a form whose submissions are never answered.
</Callout>

## Putting a form on a page

A **Form** block puts it on any page — a contact page, the bottom of a course page, a
landing page. See [Pages and blocks](/docs/knowledgebase/website/pages-and-blocks).

## Forms worth having

| Form | Where |
|---|---|
| **Contact** | Your contact page and footer |
| **Course enquiry** | On each course page, for people not ready to buy |
| **Waiting list** | On a batch that is full or not yet open |
| **Scholarship application** | Attached to a [scholarship](/docs/knowledgebase/selling-payments/scholarships) |
| **Feedback** | Required by a [certificate type](/docs/knowledgebase/certificates/certificate-types) |

The last two are not just marketing: a form can be a certificate requirement, and a
scholarship's application form is how candidates reach you.

## Common questions

<Accordions type="single">

<Accordion title="Submissions are not arriving.">
Check **Admin notifications** names somebody, and then check the
[delivery logs](/docs/knowledgebase/communication/delivery-and-analytics) — it is
usually the sending service rather than the form.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="We are getting spam.">
Turn on spam protection, and consider a field a person would answer and a machine would
not.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can a form create an account?">
No. A form collects an enquiry. Creating accounts is
[Adding people](/docs/knowledgebase/account-people/adding-people).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I export submissions?">
Submissions are listed for the form, and the list exports.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can one form be on several pages?">
Yes. Use the Form block wherever you need it.
</Accordion>

</Accordions>
