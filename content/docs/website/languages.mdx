Your site, your courses and your emails can each be written in several languages, and
visitors read the one they chose.

<WhoCan roles="Admin" />

## Enabling a language

**Enabled Languages** in your website settings, with **Add Language** to add one. A
language that is not enabled does not appear to visitors, even where text has been
written in it.

## How languages appear in addresses

**URL Structure** decides how a language shows in the address:

| Choice | Looks like |
|---|---|
| **Subdirectory** | `example.com/en/page` |
| **Subdomain** | `en.example.com/page` |
| **Query Parameter** | `example.com/page?lang=en` |

**Subdirectory** is the usual choice. It keeps one domain, reads sensibly, and search
engines handle it well.

<Callout type="warn">
Decide this before you publish. Changing it later changes every address on your site,
and each one then needs a
[redirect](/docs/knowledgebase/website/versions-and-redirects).
</Callout>

## Where translations are written

Not in one place — beside the thing being translated:

| What | Where |
|---|---|
| **Website blocks** | Each text field has a version per language. |
| **Course titles and descriptions** | The flag buttons on the course form. |
| **Lessons, quizzes, assignments** | The same flag buttons. |
| **Notices and messages** | Written per language. |

The flag buttons carry two signals worth knowing: **a small amber dot** means that
language is still empty, and **a dashed outline** means text exists in a language your
site has not enabled.

That second one is the useful warning — translated text that nobody can read because
the language is switched off.

## Fonts per language

Set a font for a specific language in your
[theme](/docs/knowledgebase/website/templates-and-themes). A Bangla-first academy should
do this: the default sans is chosen for Latin script, and Bangla reads better in a font
made for it.

## Number and date formats

Regional settings cover number format — including **১,২৩৪.৫৬ (Bangla)** — date format
(**12-hour** or **24-hour**) and **Timezone**, under **Regional Settings** in
<Path>Website > Settings > Localization</Path>. Set them to what your learners expect, not to
what the default is.

The **Timezone** does more than format dates: it decides where your academy's day starts
and ends in every date filter in the admin. Left unset, days are counted in UTC. See
[Date filters and your time zone](/docs/knowledgebase/settings/date-filters).

## Doing it properly

- **Translate the pages that sell**, first: the homepage, your main course pages,
  checkout. Not everything at once.
- **Do not half-translate a page.** A page in two languages at once reads as broken.
- **Check the layout in each language.** Bangla and Arabic text run to different lengths
  and buttons that fit in English can break.
- **Test right-to-left properly** if you enable Arabic. Everything mirrors.

## Common questions

<Accordions type="single">

<Accordion title="I translated a course and learners still see English.">
That language is not enabled, or they are reading the site in another one. The dashed
outline on the flag button tells you the first.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Do learners choose their language?">
They do, and their own **locale** is on their profile — which decides what they are
shown by default.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Do emails follow the learner's language?">
Write the version your audience reads and send to the matching audience. See
[Message templates](/docs/knowledgebase/communication/message-templates).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What happens where no translation exists?">
The text falls back to what is there — usually your main language. A partly translated
site is readable, just inconsistent.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can a course be in one language and the site in two?">
Yes. Languages are per field, so you translate what is worth translating.
</Accordion>

</Accordions>
