A learner asks for their money back. Refunds live in
<Path>Billing > Transactions > Refunds</Path>.

<WhoCan roles="Admin" />

## Full or partial

| Type | When |
|---|---|
| **Full Refund** | The whole amount. |
| **Partial Refund** | Some of it — a learner halfway through, a goodwill gesture. |

## The decision, and the reason

Every refund records **why**, from a list:

| Reason |
|---|
| Accidental purchase |
| Changed mind |
| Not as described |
| Technical issues |
| Fraudulent request |
| Other (specify below) |

Declining also takes a reason:

| Decline reason |
|---|
| Already refunded once |
| Excessive usage (more than 50% completed) |
| Outside refund window (more than 30 days) |
| Violates refund policy |
| Other |

<Callout type="info">
**The decline reasons are the shape of a refund policy.** If you do not have one
written down, these four are a reasonable starting point: one refund per learner, a
usage limit, a time window, and a policy to point at.
</Callout>

## The part people forget: access

A refund asks what happens to what they bought:

| Choice | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Revoke access** | They lose the programme. |
| **Remain existing access** | They keep it. |

Money and access are separate decisions. A learner refunded for a course that was
genuinely not as described should probably lose access; one refunded as a goodwill
gesture on a course they half finished probably should not.

**Decide this before you process the refund.** There is no undo that returns the weeks
they lost.

## Watching the pattern

The refund screens report more than individual cases:

| View | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| **Refund Rate** | What share of sales come back. |
| **Refund by Reason** | The distribution of why. |
| **Refund by Entity Type** | Which kinds of product. |
| **High Refund Rate Entities** | Specific programmes with elevated rates. |
| **Refund Trend** | Whether it is getting worse. |
| **Total Refunded (30 days)**, **Avg Refund**, **Avg Processing** | The totals, and how fast you respond. |

**High Refund Rate Entities** is the one to act on. A single course with a refund rate
far above the rest is telling you something specific — usually that its page promises
more than it delivers, or that its first week loses people. Compare it against that
programme's [statistics](/docs/knowledgebase/analytics/course-statistics).

## Common questions

<Accordions type="single">

<Accordion title="Does refunding automatically remove access?">
No. You choose — **revoke access** or **remain existing access**. Decide before you
process it.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="A learner paid in cash. Can I refund them here?">
Record the refund so the figures are right, and hand the cash back yourself. The
platform cannot return money it never received.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I refund one instalment?">
Use a partial refund for the amount actually being returned, and review their plan — a
refunded learner on a live plan will keep being billed.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="One course has a high refund rate.">
Open its statistics and look at where learners stop. A high refund rate with low
completion means the course is losing people early; with high completion it usually
means the page over-promised.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="How long do we have to refund?">
That is your policy, not a platform rule. The decline reasons suggest 30 days and 50%
completion as workable lines — write yours down and publish it.
</Accordion>

</Accordions>
