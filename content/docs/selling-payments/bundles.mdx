A bundle is several things sold together at one price. The buyer makes one purchase and
ends up enrolled in everything in it.

Bundles live in <Path>Commerce > Bundles</Path>.

<WhoCan roles="Admin" />

## Making one

| Field | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Title** | What buyers see. Required. |
| **Slug** | The short name in its web address. Required. |
| **Description** | What is in it and why it belongs together. |
| **Thumbnail** | Its cover image. |
| **Bundle Items** | The things inside — courses, paths, whatever you are selling together. |
| **Publish At** | When it goes live. Required. |
| **Status** | Draft or published. |

Then give it a price, in
[Pricing](/docs/knowledgebase/selling-payments/pricing) with the entity type **Bundle**.
Without one it cannot be bought.

## Bundle or learning path?

The distinction matters, and it is easy to get wrong:

| | **Bundle** | **Learning path** |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | A price on several things | A programme in its own right |
| The learner ends up with | Separate enrolments | One enrolment |
| Progress | Per item | One figure for the path |
| Result and certificate | Per item | One for the path |
| Order | None | Stages, and prerequisites if you want them |

**Use a bundle when** the items are independent and the offer is commercial — "these
three courses, 20% cheaper together".

**Use a [learning path](/docs/knowledgebase/courses/learning-paths) when** the items
only make sense together — a diploma, a career track, a syllabus.

If a learner should feel they joined *one* thing, it is a path.

## The one-item rule

Checkout takes one item per purchase. A bundle **is** one item, which is exactly why it
exists: without it, "buy these three" would be three separate purchases.

## Common questions

<Accordions type="single">

<Accordion title="A learner already owns one course in the bundle.">
They are enrolled in the rest; the one they have is unaffected. There is no partial
price for the overlap.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can a bundle contain another bundle?">
Put the items in directly. A bundle of bundles makes the price impossible to reason
about.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Does a bundle award its own certificate?">
No. Each item inside awards its own, under its own rules. For one certificate across
several courses, use a learning path.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I bundle a cohort-based course?">
Add the batch rather than the course, since the batch is what learners enrol into. Be
careful with dates: a bundle selling in March containing a batch that started in
January will refuse at checkout.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I change a bundle's items after people have bought it?">
Existing buyers keep what they were enrolled in. New buyers get the new list. Adding an
item does not give it to people who bought before.
</Accordion>

</Accordions>
