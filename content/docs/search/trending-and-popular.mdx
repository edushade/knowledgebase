Trending searches are the terms people are using on your site right now. They appear in
the search box before anybody has typed anything.

<WhoCan roles="Admin, Learner" />

## What visitors see

Open the search box and, before typing, a visitor sees what is trending — along with
their own recent searches.

It is a small thing that does two jobs: it gives somebody who does not know what to look
for a starting point, and it tells them other people are using the site.

## Why you should read it

Trending searches are the most honest market research your academy has. Unlike a survey,
nobody is being polite.

Three questions it answers:

| What you see | What it means |
|---|---|
| A term you teach, trending | Promote that course. Demand is already there. |
| A term you **do not** teach, trending | A course you could build, asked for by name. |
| A term you teach, searched with **no results** | Your title and keywords are hiding it. |

The third is the cheapest win in this whole knowledge base. The course exists; people
want it; they cannot find it. Add the term they typed to the course's **Search
Keywords** — see [How search works](/docs/knowledgebase/search/search-basics).

## A monthly habit

Once a month, look at what is trending and ask of each term: do we teach this, and can
it be found? Ten minutes, and it usually produces one keyword fix and one course idea.

## Common questions

<Accordions type="single">

<Accordion title="Nothing is trending.">
Not enough searches yet. Trending needs traffic.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I choose what is shown as trending?">
No. It reflects what people actually search for, which is the point.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Someone searched for something embarrassing.">
Trending reflects volume, so a one-off does not surface. Terms that trend are terms
several people used.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I see searches that returned nothing?">
That is the list worth asking for. Start with what is trending and test those terms
yourself — a term that trends and returns nothing is a gap you can close today.
</Accordion>
