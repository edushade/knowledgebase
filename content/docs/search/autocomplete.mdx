As a learner types into the search box, suggestions appear. It is the most-used part of
search and the part nobody configures — because it works from what you have already
written.

<WhoCan roles="Learner" />

## What appears

| As they type | They see |
|---|---|
| Nothing yet | Their [recent searches](/docs/knowledgebase/search/search-history) and what is [trending](/docs/knowledgebase/search/trending-and-popular) |
| A few characters | Matching courses and suggested searches |
| Something with no matches | **Did you mean…**, with a closer spelling |

Suggestions start after a couple of characters, so a single letter does not produce
noise.

## Why it matters more than the results page

Most learners never reach the results page. They type three characters, see the thing
they wanted, and click it.

That means the first few words of a course title do almost all the work. "Spoken English
— Beginner" shows usefully after three characters; "Module 1: An Introduction to
Communication" does not.

## Making suggestions better

1. **Front-load titles.** The distinguishing word first.
2. **Fill in search keywords.** They feed suggestions, and they are never shown — see
   [How search works](/docs/knowledgebase/search/search-basics).
3. **Use the words learners use.** "IELTS", not "International English Language Testing
   System".

## Common questions

<Accordions type="single">

<Accordion title="Nothing appears as I type.">
Suggestions need a couple of characters. If still nothing, check that published,
matching content exists.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I pin a course to the top?">
No. Suggestions come from what matches.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I switch autocomplete off?">
It is part of the search bar on your site.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Why does a course not appear until I type its full name?">
Its distinguishing word is late in the title. Rename it so the useful word comes first.
</Accordion>
