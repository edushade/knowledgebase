Search is how most visitors find a course. It sits on your website, and what it can find
is decided by what you put into your courses.

<WhoCan roles="Learner" />

## What is searched

A search looks across your courses, learning paths, products and other content, matching
on their titles, descriptions, categories and tags.

Results show what the thing is, its difficulty, and — for a quiz — how many questions,
minutes and attempts it carries. A search that finds nothing offers **Did you mean…**
with a closer spelling.

## Making a course findable

Three things, in order of how much difference they make:

### 1. The title

Learners search for what they want, not for what you called it. "Spoken English for
Professionals" is found; "Communication Skills — Module A" is not.

### 2. Search keywords

On the course's settings step, **Search Keywords** holds "other names people might
search for, such as an abbreviation or its name in another language". **They are never
shown to learners** — they only widen what finds the course.

<Callout type="info">
**This is the field most academies never fill in, and it costs them.** A learner
searching "ইংরেজি" or "IELTS prep" or "MS Excel" finds nothing if your course is called
"Business English". Put the alternative names in keywords.
</Callout>

### 3. Categories and tags

Both are searched and both become filters. See
[Categories and tags](/docs/knowledgebase/account-people/categories-and-tags).

## Searching from the admin side

Staff have their own search, and it is a different thing: the box in the top bar of
every admin screen, or **Ctrl K** from anywhere.

It searches **People** and **Content** together and labels what each result is — Course,
Learning Path, and so on — so "find that learner" and "find that course" are the same
keystroke. It is the fastest way around the admin panel, and most staff never discover
it.

This search is not affected by the keywords below; it is for your team, not your
visitors.

## What search does not reach

- **Unpublished content.** Draft courses are not findable.
- **Lesson content.** Search covers the things you offer, not the words inside a video.
- **Content a learner cannot see.** Access rules apply to search as they do everywhere.

## Watching what people search for

Searches are recorded, which gives you two useful things:

- **[Trending searches](/docs/knowledgebase/search/trending-and-popular)** — what people
  are looking for right now.
- **Searches with no results** — what people want that you do not offer, or that your
  titles and keywords are hiding.

The second is a course roadmap written by your market, and almost nobody reads it.

## Common questions

<Accordions type="single">

<Accordion title="A course exists but search cannot find it.">
Check it is published. Then check the words a learner would actually type are in its
title, description, tags or search keywords.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Learners search in Bangla and find nothing.">
Put the Bangla name in **Search Keywords**. Keywords are not shown, so there is no cost
to adding them.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can learners search inside a course?">
Search covers your catalogue. Inside a course, the outline is how learners find their
way.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Does search respect access rules?">
Yes. Learners do not find content they could not open.
</Accordion>

</Accordions>
