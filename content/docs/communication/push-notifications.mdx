A push notification appears on a learner's device without them opening anything. It is
the most immediate channel you have, and the easiest to ruin.

<WhoCan roles="Admin" />

## Setting it up

Push is carried by **FCM** or **APNs**, connected in
<Path>Notifications > Settings</Path> like any other sending service. See
[Channels](/docs/knowledgebase/communication/channels).

Alongside the credentials you set defaults:

| Setting | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Default Icon URL** | The icon shown with a push. |
| **Default Badge URL** | The badge. |

Use your own logo. A default icon on a notification is a notification from nobody.

## Learners have to agree

A learner is asked whether to allow push notifications. Many say no, and once they have,
asking again is awkward.

<Callout type="info">
**Ask at the right moment.** A permission prompt the second somebody arrives is declined
out of reflex. Asked after they enrol, when they have a reason to want reminders, far
more say yes.
</Callout>

## What push is good for, and not

| Good for | Not for |
|---|---|
| A deadline tomorrow | Marketing |
| A live class starting in 15 minutes | Anything that can wait |
| A result published | Routine course updates |
| An overdue payment | Anything you would not interrupt someone for |

The test: would you tap somebody on the shoulder to tell them this?

## Rate limits and quiet hours

Push has its own marketing limits — **Marketing Push per Day** and **per Week** — and
quiet hours apply to it like any other channel. Push is the channel most likely to wake
somebody, so both matter more here than anywhere else. See
[Quiet hours](/docs/knowledgebase/communication/quiet-hours).

## Writing one

A title and a line. The whole thing is read on a lock screen in a second, so:

- **The title carries the news.** "Assignment due tomorrow", not "Reminder".
- **The line says what to do.** "Submit before 11pm."
- **It links somewhere specific** — the assignment, not the dashboard.

## Common questions

<Accordions type="single">

<Accordion title="Nobody is receiving push notifications.">
Check the service is connected and enabled, then whether learners actually granted
permission. A declined permission is silent on your side.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="A learner declined and wants it now.">
They re-enable it in their device settings for your site — their browser or phone, not
your platform.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Does push work without a mobile app?">
Browsers support it, with the learner's permission. A mobile app makes it more reliable.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="How many is too many?">
More than a few a week and people switch it off — permanently. Use it for things that
genuinely cannot wait.
</Accordion>

</Accordions>
