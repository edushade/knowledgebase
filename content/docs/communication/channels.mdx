A **channel** is how a message travels. A **sending service** is the company that
carries it. Nothing leaves your platform until at least one service is connected.

<Path>Notifications > Settings</Path>.

<WhoCan roles="Admin" />

## The channels

| Channel | Carried by |
|---|---|
| **Email** | SendGrid, Mailgun, Cloudflare |
| **SMS** | Twilio, Plivo, Vonage |
| **WhatsApp** | Twilio |
| **Push** | FCM, APNs |
| **In-app** | Nothing to connect — it is your platform |
| **Slack**, **Discord**, **Telegram** | Those services |

Email and SMS are what an academy actually needs. The chat channels are for notifying
your own team — a Slack message when a payment fails, say — rather than for reaching
learners.

## Connecting one

Each service takes its own credentials, plus:

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Enabled** | Whether it is used. |
| **Priority** | Lower number wins. With two email services configured, the lower priority is tried first. |
| **Rate limits** | **Per minute**, **per hour**, **per day**. |

<Callout type="warn">
**Secrets are saved and hidden.** Leave a credential blank to keep the current value, or
type a new one to replace it — you cannot read the old one back.
</Callout>

## Test it before you need it

Every service has **Send Test Notification**: an email address or a phone number, and a
message sent to it. There is also a connection test that checks the credentials without
sending anything.

<Callout type="error">
**Do this before an intake, not during one.** An academy that discovers its email
service is misconfigured on registration day loses that intake's registrations — every
verification email, every welcome, every password reset.
</Callout>

## Rate limits

Set them to what your service actually allows. Exceeded limits mean messages that do not
arrive, and a campaign that fails halfway is worse than one you did not send.

## Priority, and a fallback

With two services on the same channel, priority decides which is tried first. It is also
a fallback: if your main email service has a bad day, the second carries the messages.

For an academy where a missed verification email means a lost sale, a second email
service is cheap insurance.

## Common questions

<Accordions type="single">

<Accordion title="No emails are being sent at all.">
Check that an email service is connected and **Enabled**, then run the connection test,
then send a test notification. One of those three tells you where it stops.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Some messages arrive and some do not.">
Look at your rate limits first, then
[Delivery and analytics](/docs/knowledgebase/communication/delivery-and-analytics) for
what each message actually did.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Do I need SMS?">
In Bangladesh, often yes. Learners who never open email read a text. It costs per
message, so use it for things that matter — verification, payment, a deadline.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can learners choose what they receive?">
Yes, per channel, in their own settings. See
[Notification preferences](/docs/knowledgebase/communication/notification-preferences).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What is the in-app channel?">
Notifications inside your platform, in the learner's bell. Nothing to connect, nothing
to pay for, and the only channel that cannot fail to deliver.
</Accordion>

</Accordions>
