A campaign is one message sent to a group. A reminder to everyone who has not started, a
notice to a batch, an offer to learners who finished last term.

<Path>Notifications > Campaigns</Path>.

<WhoCan roles="Admin" />

## Sending one

Five steps, in one dialog. **New Campaign** on the Campaigns screen starts it, and you
can go **Back** at any point without losing what you have filled in.

<Screenshot
  src="/kb/communication/new-campaign.png"
  alt="Step 1 of the campaign dialog, with campaign name and the three campaign types."
  caption="Step 1 of 5. The progress dots at the top tell you how far through you are."
/>

<Steps>

<Step>

### Campaign Details

A **Campaign Name**, a **Campaign Type**, and optionally a **Description** and **Tags**.

The name is for your own list, not for learners — in six months "Reminder 3" tells you
nothing.

The type is the part that matters:

| Type | What it is for |
|---|---|
| **Transactional** | Automated messages triggered by what someone did. |
| **Marketing** | Promotions and newsletters. |
| **Announcement** | Important updates and notifications. |

<Callout type="warn">
**Mislabelling an offer as Transactional sends marketing to people who opted out.**
Learners can switch marketing off and still receive transactional messages — that is the
whole point of the distinction, and it is the one setting here that can get you into
trouble.
</Callout>

</Step>

<Step>

### Select Audience

Either **Use Existing Segment** and pick one, or **Create New Segment** and define it
here. See [Audiences](/docs/knowledgebase/communication/audiences).

Once you pick one, the dialog shows the **Estimated Audience Size** — the number of
people this will reach.

<Screenshot
  src="/kb/communication/campaign-audience.png"
  alt="Step 2 of the campaign dialog, with a segment chosen and an estimated audience size of 330 users."
  caption="Read the number before you go on. A campaign to nobody and a campaign to everybody look identical until you look."
/>

The screen warns that *"Audience size may change by send time based on segment
criteria"*, and it means it. An audience defined by conditions is recounted when the
campaign actually goes out, so a campaign scheduled for next week reaches whoever
qualifies next week — not the people who qualify today.

</Step>

<Step>

### Template & Channels

Pick a **template**, then tick the **channels** it goes out on. The two are one step
because the template decides what is available.

<Screenshot
  src="/kb/communication/campaign-channels.png"
  alt="The channel list, with Email, Push Notification and SMS ticked and WhatsApp greyed out as Not configured."
  caption="A channel the template has no version for cannot be ticked. One that your academy has not connected shows Not configured."
/>

Each channel is marked:

- **Available in template** — the template has a version for it, so you can tick it.
- **Not configured** — your academy has not connected that service. See
  [Channels](/docs/knowledgebase/communication/channels).

**You can only select channels configured in the template.** If you want to send by SMS
and the option is greyed out, the fix is in the template, not here — add an SMS version
to it and come back.

Push is worth understanding: it reaches phones with the app installed, and everyone else
in the audience who has an account sees the same message in their notification bell
instead. Nobody is missed because they have no phone.

</Step>

<Step>

### Schedule

**When to Send**, and the options that keep a campaign from annoying people.

<Screenshot
  src="/kb/communication/campaign-schedule.png"
  alt="Step 4, showing Send Immediately, Schedule for Later and Recurring, with sending options below."
  caption="The three sending options below the schedule are all on sensible defaults. Leave them alone unless you have a reason."
/>

| When to Send | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Send Immediately** | Starts as soon as you confirm. |
| **Schedule for Later** | A specific date and time. |
| **Recurring** | A repeating schedule. |

Then **Sending Options**:

| Option | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Respect user quiet hours** | On | Nothing is delivered during the hours a learner marked as quiet. See [Quiet hours](/docs/knowledgebase/communication/quiet-hours). |
| **Skip users who received notification recently** | On, 24 hours | Anyone already messaged inside that window is left out of this one. |
| **A/B Test (send to 10% sample first)** | Off | Sends to a tenth of the audience before everyone. |

That second one is the quiet hero. Two campaigns scheduled on the same morning would
otherwise both land on the same people; with it on, the second skips anyone the first
already reached.

</Step>

<Step>

### Review & Confirm

Everything on one card — name, type, audience and its size, template and its version,
channels, schedule — and an **Estimated Cost** before you commit.

<Screenshot
  src="/kb/communication/campaign-review.png"
  alt="The review card, showing audience, template, channels, schedule and an estimated cost broken down per channel."
  caption="The cost is worked out per channel and per recipient. SMS is where the money goes."
/>

The cost is broken down by channel, so you can see exactly where it comes from. Email
and push are fractions of a penny each; SMS is not. On a 330-person audience that
difference is the whole bill.

If the number surprises you, go **Back** and untick SMS.

</Step>

</Steps>

## Priority

A campaign also carries a **Priority** — **High**, **Medium** or **Low**. It decides what
goes first when several campaigns are queued at once. Medium is right for almost
everything; save High for something genuinely time-bound, like a cancelled class.

## After it has gone

Each campaign has its own analytics: sent, delivered, not delivered, and the errors. See
[Delivery and analytics](/docs/knowledgebase/communication/delivery-and-analytics).

## Campaigns worth running

| Audience | Message |
|---|---|
| Enrolled, never started | "Your course is waiting — here is the first lesson." |
| Started, nothing for 14 days | "You stopped at lesson 4. Ten minutes gets you to 5." |
| Access expiring in 7 days | "Your access ends on the 14th." |
| Finished a course | "You have finished — here is what comes next." |
| Instalment 1–7 days late | A gentle reminder. |

Each of those is an audience you can build from conditions, and each recovers learners
or money that would otherwise be lost quietly.

## Common questions

<Accordions type="single">

<Accordion title="My campaign went to nobody.">
The audience matched nobody. Step 2 shows the **Estimated Audience Size** — read it
before going on. A condition on a field most learners have never filled in excludes
almost everyone.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Some learners did not receive it.">
Three causes, in order: their notification preferences, a rate limit, or a delivery
failure. The delivery logs say which.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I send to one person?">
A campaign is for groups. For one learner, message them directly or use a notice.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I stop a campaign mid-send?">
A scheduled campaign can be paused before it starts. Once sending has begun, what has
gone has gone.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Does a campaign cost money?">
SMS and WhatsApp do, per message, through your sending service. Email and push are
fractions of a penny. The last step shows an **Estimated Cost** broken down per channel
before you confirm, so you never find out afterwards.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can a campaign repeat?">
Yes — **Recurring** on the schedule step sets up a repeating send. Be careful pairing it
with an audience defined by conditions: each run recounts the audience, which is usually
what you want, but it means the list is not the same every time.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="The channel I want is greyed out.">
Either the template has no version for that channel, or your academy has not connected
the service. The label says which: **Not configured** means the service, anything else
means the template. Add the missing version to the template and the tick box comes back.
</Accordion>

</Accordions>
