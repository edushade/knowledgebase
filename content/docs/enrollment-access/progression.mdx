At the end of a semester, somebody has to decide who goes on. Progression is that
decision, made once as a rule for the semester and then overridden for the individuals
who need it.

It applies to batches and learning paths divided into
[terms](/docs/knowledgebase/courses/terms-and-breakpoints), and it has its own
**Progression** tab, beside **Learner Grades**.

<Callout type="info">
**No Progression tab?** It only appears where the programme is divided into semesters —
without them there is nothing to progress between, so the tab is not offered rather than
offered and then apologised for. Add
[terms](/docs/knowledgebase/courses/terms-and-breakpoints) first.
</Callout>

<WhoCan roles="Admin" />

## The rule counts subjects, not a percentage

A semester's progression rule says **how many of that semester's subjects a learner must
pass** to go on to the next one.

That is deliberate, and it is not the same as a pass mark. A learner can clear the
overall pass mark while failing most of their subjects — strong in two, absent in four.
Counting subjects is what catches that.

Set it on the [weights page](/docs/knowledgebase/progress-grading/weighted-grading),
beside the semester whose subjects you are weighting, because that is where you are
already deciding what the semester is made of. The control reads **Progression** until
you set a number, and **Pass 4 to continue** once you have.

| Field | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Subjects that must be passed** | The number, shown against how many the semester holds — "4 of 6 in this semester". |
| **Rule in effect** | Switch it off to suspend the rule for this intake without losing the number. |

Two rules the screen enforces for you:

- **Bonus subjects are not counted.** Only subjects marked Full are in the total, so the
  "of 6" beside the number is what the rule will actually measure.
- **A subject not yet sat is not a pass.** Failed and never-attempted count the same way.

<Callout type="warn">
**You cannot require more passes than the semester has counted subjects.** Asking for
five out of four would hold back every learner in it, so the screen refuses the number
rather than producing a semester nobody leaves.

Leave it empty, or set 0, and everyone continues.
</Callout>

## The roster

The **Progression** tab lists everyone in a semester with the working behind each
verdict. Pick the semester at the top; filter to a **Programme** first, because the
roster is worked out per programme.

| Column | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| **Learner** | Who. |
| **Standing** | Which of the six buckets below they are in. |
| **Subjects passed** | How many counted subjects they have passed. |
| **Still to pass** | How many are left — failed and never-sat alike. |
| **Programme** | Whether the whole programme is still within reach. |
| **Next semester** | Whether they already hold it. |

### The six standings

| Standing | What it means | What it needs |
|---|---|---|
| **May continue** | They met the rule. | Give them the next semester. |
| **Held by the rule** | They did not meet it. | A decision, or another term. |
| **Admitted by decision** | Somebody moved them on despite the rule. | Nothing — but the reason is on the record. |
| **Held by decision** | Somebody held them back despite the rule. | Same. |
| **Not gated** | This semester has no rule, so nobody is held. | Nothing. |
| **No access to this semester** | They do not hold this semester at all, so the counts beside them are not about them. | Access to *this* semester, not the next one. |

<Callout type="info">
**"Held by the rule" and "No access to this semester" look similar and need opposite
things.** One is a learner who sat the semester and fell short — that needs a decision.
The other is a learner who was never given the semester in the first place — that needs
access. Reading them as the same thing is how people end up admitting someone to a
semester they have not started.
</Callout>

### The Programme column

This one answers a different question: can this learner still finish the whole
programme?

| It says | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Can finish the programme** | Every subject the programme counts has been passed. |
| **Subjects still outstanding** | Something counted is still unpassed. |
| **Not graded yet** | Not enough has been marked to tell. |

<Callout type="warn">
**This is not "overall 50% or more".** It is the certificate's own rule — every counted
subject passed. A learner can clear the overall mark on bonus subjects while failing
core ones, and these two answers must never disagree. See
[Certificate requirements](/docs/knowledgebase/certificates/certificate-requirements).
</Callout>

### Narrowing it down

Filter by **Standing**, by **Programme**, by **Next semester** (**Already has access**
or **No access yet**), and by the counts:

- **Subjects passed, at least** / **at most**
- **Still to pass, at least** / **at most**

"Still to pass, at most 1" is the list worth building first: the learners one subject
away, who are usually worth a conversation rather than a repeated year.

## Decisions for individuals

The rule covers the ordinary case. For anyone else, record a decision:

| Action | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Admit** | Admit to the next semester, whatever the rule says. |
| **Hold back** | Hold back at this semester, whatever the rule says. |
| **Remove decision** | Put them back on the semester's rule. |

Every decision asks **why this person is being treated differently from the rule**, and
the answer is kept with it. Six months later, when somebody asks why a learner skipped a
semester, the answer is on the record rather than in somebody's memory.

A decision stands whatever the rule later becomes. If you change the rule mid-year and
want a learner judged by the new one, remove their decision.

### Doing it for many at once

Select learners — or select everything matching your filters — and record one decision
with one reason for all of them. The bulk action resolves **the same filters the list is
showing**, so what you act on is exactly what you were looking at.

It reports back in people, not rows: *"14 people updated, 14 people given the next
semester."* Decisions and access are counted separately on purpose — a run can record
every decision and still fail to grant a single semester, and those are two very
different mornings.

## What progression actually does

Admitting a learner gives them access to the next semester's content. It is a change to
what they can open — see
[Partial access](/docs/knowledgebase/enrollment-access/partial-access), which is the
mechanism underneath.

It does not change their marks, re-mark anything, or issue a certificate.

## Common questions

<Accordions type="single">

<Accordion title="A learner passed everything but has no access to the next semester.">
Progression is not automatic. The rule describes who *qualifies*; somebody still grants
the semester. Filter the roster to **May continue** and **No access yet** — that is
exactly the list of people waiting on you.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="The roster says a learner still has subjects to pass, but they say they passed.">
Open their grades and check at the subject level. A subject counts as passed when its
result falls in a band marked as passing — so the pass line in your
[grading scheme](/docs/knowledgebase/progress-grading/grading-schemes) is what decides
it, and [rounding](/docs/knowledgebase/progress-grading/rounding-and-precision) can move
a borderline result either way.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Why does it say a bonus subject does not count?">
Because a bonus subject adds to a result rather than forming part of it. If bonus
subjects counted towards progression, a learner could continue on the strength of an
optional subject while failing the core ones. See
[Weighted grading](/docs/knowledgebase/progress-grading/weighted-grading).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="There is nothing to grant on the last semester.">
Correct — the final semester of a batch has no successor, so there is nothing after it
to give. What matters there is the **Programme** column and the
[certificate](/docs/knowledgebase/certificates/certificate-requirements).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can a learner repeat one subject rather than the whole semester?">
Progression works at the semester level. To let someone re-sit one subject, give them
another attempt at its assessments instead — see
[Attempts and retakes](/docs/knowledgebase/assessments/attempts-and-retakes).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="We suspended the rule for this intake. What happens to the roster?">
Everyone shows as **Not gated**, and nobody is held. The number you typed is still
there, so switching **Rule in effect** back on restores it exactly.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Where do I see why a learner was held back?">
On their row in the roster. The reason typed when the decision was made is kept with it.
</Accordion>

</Accordions>
