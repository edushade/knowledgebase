---
title: "Prerequisites"
description: "Making one piece of content wait until another is finished — and how to set it without locking everybody out."
---

A prerequisite says: this opens when that is done. Module 2 after Module 1, the final
exam after every assignment, the advanced course after the foundation one.

It is one of the six [drip strategies](/docs/knowledgebase/enrollment-access/drip-scheduling) —
**Completion Based** — and it is set from a programme's **Prerequisites** tab or an
item's **⋯** menu.

<WhoCan roles="Admin, Instructor" />

## Setting one

1. Open the item that should wait.
2. Choose **Completion Based** as its unlock strategy.
3. Pick what must be finished first.

The item then stays locked for a learner until they have completed the thing it depends
on.

## "Finished" means whatever you said it means

A prerequisite is satisfied when the item it points at counts as **complete** — and what
counts as complete is that item's
[completion rule](/docs/knowledgebase/progress-grading/completion-rules).

That matters more than it sounds:

| The prerequisite item's rule | The learner gets through by |
|---|---|
| **Viewed / Opened** | Opening it. |
| **Reach a percentage** | Watching or reading that far. |
| **Attempt only** | Sitting the quiz — pass or fail. |
| **Attempted with a Pass** | Passing it. |

<Callout type="warn">
**A pass-only prerequisite with limited attempts can trap a learner.** If the gate quiz
must be passed, and it allows two attempts, a learner who fails twice has nowhere to go.
Before you set it, check how many attempts the quiz allows, and decide what you will do
for the learner who does not make it.
</Callout>

## Prerequisites between courses

A learning path can require one course before another: the second course's
prerequisite is the first. A learner who skipped the foundation course finds the
advanced one locked, with what they must finish first.

See [Learning paths](/docs/knowledgebase/courses/learning-paths).

## What the learner sees

The item, **locked**, with what they must complete first. Unlike an
[eligibility rule](/docs/knowledgebase/enrollment-access/eligibility-rules), a
prerequisite tells them — which is the point. A gate nobody can see is just a broken
course.

## Removing them

**Remove all prerequisites** on a programme deletes its saved rule, after which
everyone can reach the content. It cannot be undone.

## Common questions

<Accordions type="single">

<Accordion title="A learner finished the prerequisite but the next item is still locked.">
Check that the prerequisite item is actually ticked for them, on the programme's
**Progress** tab. The usual cause is a completion rule they have not quite met — a
video whose duration is set longer than the real video, for instance.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="A learner failed the gate quiz and cannot continue.">
Either give them another attempt, or set a makeup assessment that satisfies the same
slot. See [Attempts and retakes](/docs/knowledgebase/assessments/attempts-and-retakes)
and [Alternative assessments](/docs/knowledgebase/assessments/alternative-assessments).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can one item depend on several others?">
Yes — a final exam can wait on every assignment in the course. Be careful with long
chains: one unticked item near the start holds up everything after it.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Do prerequisites change a learner's progress figure?">
No. Locked content still counts in the denominator, so a learner at the start of a
gated course is near 0%, not 100%.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What is the difference between a prerequisite and an eligibility rule?">
A prerequisite is about **what they have done**; an eligibility rule is about **who they
are**. A prerequisite tells the learner what to finish; an eligibility rule says
nothing at all.
</Accordion>

</Accordions>
