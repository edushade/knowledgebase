title: "Drip Scheduling"
description: "Releasing content over time instead of all at once — the six strategies, item overrides, and extending a deadline for one learner."
---

Drip scheduling decides **when** content opens. A course can hand everything over on day
one, release a module a week, or hold a final exam until a fixed date.

It lives on the **Drip Schedule** tab of a programme's builder, and on each item's **⋯**
menu for the exceptions.

<WhoCan roles="Admin, Instructor" />

## The six strategies

**Content Unlock Strategy** decides how content opens:

| Strategy | When content opens |
|---|---|
| **Immediate** | Straight away. Content is available as soon as a learner enrols. |
| **Fixed Date** | On a date and time you set. Everyone gets it at the same moment. |
| **After Enrollment** | A set period after *that learner* enrolled — "two weeks in", counted from their own start. |
| **After Unlock** | A set period after the previous item opened, so the course walks forward item by item. |
| **Completion Based** | After the learner finishes something else. See [Prerequisites](/docs/knowledgebase/enrollment-access/prerequisites). |
| **Custom** | A schedule you build item by item. |

Alongside the unlock you can set a **lock** — when content closes again — or **No Lock**
to leave it open once it has opened.

## Fixed Date or After Enrollment

This is the choice people get wrong, and the difference matters:

- **Fixed Date** suits a cohort. Everyone reaches week three in the same week, so a live
  session and a deadline make sense.
- **After Enrollment** suits self-paced learners. Someone who joins in March gets their
  week three in March, not alongside the January intake.

Using Fixed Date on a self-paced course means a learner who buys in December finds half
the course already "released" and the rest at dates that mean nothing to them.

## Item-level overrides

The strategy covers the programme. **Item-Level Overrides** handle the exceptions —
"everything opens weekly, but the final exam is on 14 June".

Click an item in the curriculum to configure it, or select several and set them
together. Overrides are listed so you can see at a glance which items are not following
the programme rule.

## Per-learner re-schedules

**User-level Overrides** re-schedule content for named learners — an extended deadline,
a late joiner catching up, an illness.

Add an override, pick the learners, and set their unlock and lock dates. Remove it and
they go back to the default schedule.

<Callout type="info">
This is the right tool for "can I have another week?". It keeps the deadline real for
everyone else, and it leaves a record of who was given extra time.
</Callout>

## What the learner sees

Content held by a drip schedule shows as **locked**, with the date it will open. That is
different from content held by an
[eligibility rule](/docs/knowledgebase/enrollment-access/eligibility-rules), which does
not appear at all.

So: drip says "not yet". Eligibility says nothing.

## Clearing a schedule

**Clear all drip schedules** removes every schedule on the programme and returns
everything to immediately available. There are narrower versions — remove the schedules
for selected items, all item overrides, or all user overrides — and each asks before it
acts. None can be undone.

## Common questions

<Accordions type="single">

<Accordion title="A learner cannot see content that should have opened.">
Work down [How access works together](/docs/knowledgebase/enrollment-access/how-access-works-together).
If the item shows as locked with a date, it is the drip schedule; if it is not there at
all, it is not drip.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Everyone sees different content on the same day.">
The strategy is **After Enrollment**, so each learner's clock starts when they enrolled.
Switch to **Fixed Date** if the batch should move together.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="I set a lock date and learners lost work.">
Locking closes content. Anything already finished stays finished, but they cannot go
back in. Use **No Lock** unless the closing is the point — an exam window, for example.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I drip a whole section at once?">
Yes. Set the schedule on the section and its contents follow it, then override the
individual items that need different dates.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Does drip affect progress or grades?">
No. Unopened content still counts in the denominator — a learner three weeks into a
twelve-week course is three twelfths through, not 100%. See
[Progress tracking](/docs/knowledgebase/progress-grading/progress-tracking).
</Accordion>

</Accordions>
