An eligibility rule answers *who*. Not when, not after what — who. "Only learners in
Bangladesh." "Only people already enrolled in the foundation course." "Only learners
whose profile says they are over 18."

Rules live on the **Prerequisites** tab of a programme, and on an item's **⋯** menu
under **Access Rules**.

<WhoCan roles="Admin" />

## Building a rule

A rule is one or more conditions. Each condition is a **field**, an **operator** and a
**value** — "Country is one of Bangladesh, India".

Fields are grouped so they are findable, in categories such as **User Profile**,
**Classification**, **Contact Info**, **Enrollment**, **Activity** and **Preferences**.
The ones you will reach for most:

| Field | Useful for |
|---|---|
| **Enrolled in Course** / **Learning Path** / **Cohort** | Requiring a learner to already be in something. |
| **Total Enrollments** | Rules for learners with more than one programme. |
| **Country** / **Continent** | Regional pricing, regional cohorts. |
| **Gender** | Separate sections where your institution runs them. |
| **Is Learner** / **Is Instructor** | Limiting content to staff. |
| **Last Seen** / **First Seen** | Reaching dormant or new learners. |
| **Email Verified**, **Status** | Keeping unverified accounts out. |

Operators follow the kind of field: text fields offer *equals*, *contains*, *is one of*;
numbers offer *greater than*, *between*; dates offer *before*, *in the last*; and so on.

<Callout type="info">
These are the same fields the notification
[audiences](/docs/knowledgebase/communication/audiences) are built from. A rule you have
worked out for one is a rule you can reason about in the other.
</Callout>

## Preview before you save

**Eligible Users Preview** shows who the rule currently lets in. Use it — a rule that
reads correctly and matches nobody is the most common mistake, and the preview catches
it in a second.

With no rules at all, the preview has nothing to show, because everyone is eligible.

## The honest limitation

**A learner is never told why they cannot see something.** Restricted content simply
does not appear.

That is good for privacy and bad for support, because from the learner's side there is
no difference between "you are not eligible" and "this course has no content". If a rule
is likely to exclude people who will ask, say so somewhere they can read — the course
description, or an [announcement](/docs/knowledgebase/communication/announcements).

## Removing a rule

**Remove all prerequisites** deletes the saved rule, after which everyone can reach the
content. It cannot be undone, and the screen says so before you confirm.

## Eligibility is not drip

They are easy to confuse and behave quite differently:

| | **Eligibility rule** | **Drip schedule** |
|---|---|---|
| Asks | Who | When |
| A held item | Does not appear | Appears, locked, with its date |
| Changes when | The learner's details change | The clock moves |

See [Drip scheduling](/docs/knowledgebase/enrollment-access/drip-scheduling).

## Common questions

<Accordions type="single">

<Accordion title="My rule matches nobody.">
Open **Eligible Users Preview**. The usual causes are conditions that have to be true at
once when you meant either/or, and fields that are empty for most of your learners —
a rule on Gender excludes everyone who never filled it in.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="A learner says the course is empty.">
That is what an eligibility rule looks like from their side. Check the rules on the
programme and on its items, and check the preview for whether that learner is in it.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can a rule stop someone buying a course?">
Rules govern access to content. To close a programme to purchase, use **Enrollment
Open** on its settings, or an
[enrolment window](/docs/knowledgebase/enrollment-access/enrollment-windows).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="A learner met the rule and still sees nothing.">
Something else is holding it. Work down
[How access works together](/docs/knowledgebase/enrollment-access/how-access-works-together)
— partial access and publishing both cause this.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Do rules apply to admins?">
Rules govern the content a person can open. Your own view of the course from the builder
is not the learner's view — always check as a learner before deciding a rule is broken.
</Accordion>

</Accordions>
