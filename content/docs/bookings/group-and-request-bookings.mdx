Most bookings are one person picking a free slot. Two variations change that, and they
are set on the [meeting plan](/docs/knowledgebase/bookings/meeting-plans).

<WhoCan roles="Admin, Instructor" />

## Group bookings

**Meeting Plan Type** of **One to Many** makes a slot something several people join.

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Group Meeting Spots** | How many places in a slot. |
| **Max Participants Limit** | The ceiling. |
| **Guests Allowed** | Whether a booker may bring others. |

Good for a group tutorial, a shared office hour, a revision session. The booking page
shows how many places are left.

<Callout type="info">
**Group bookings are not a webinar.** For one session many people register for at a time
you set, use an [event](/docs/knowledgebase/bookings/events). A group booking is still a
slot somebody picks.
</Callout>

## Request-based bookings

**Booking Mode** of **Request-Based Booking**: "customers request a time, and you must
approve it before it's confirmed."

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1 | The invitee asks for a time. |
| 2 | The request arrives for you, and the menu shows a badge. |
| 3 | You accept their time, or offer another. |
| 4 | Confirmed, and they are told. |

Opening a request lets you "review the invitee's preferred times or select a new time".

### When it is worth the extra step

- Meetings you may need to decline.
- Meetings needing the right person, not just any slot.
- Admissions interviews, assessments, anything with a gate.

### When it is not

Anything routine. Every step loses people, and an unanswered request loses them
completely — they do not know whether to wait.

<Callout type="warn">
Request-based bookings create a queue somebody has to work daily. If nobody owns that
queue, use instant booking.
</Callout>

## Paid and request-based

A plan can be both: they request, you approve, they pay, it is confirmed. **Pending
booking expiry** decides how long the slot is held between approval and payment. See
[Booking payments](/docs/knowledgebase/bookings/payments).

Three steps is a lot. Use it only where approval genuinely matters.

## Common questions

<Accordions type="single">

<Accordion title="A group slot shows as full with places left.">
Check **Group Meeting Spots** against **Max Participants Limit** — the lower of the two
is what applies.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Requests are piling up.">
Nobody is working the queue. Either assign it to somebody, or switch the plan to instant
booking.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I decline a request?">
Yes, and the invitee is told. Declining is better than leaving it.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can guests join without booking?">
**Guests Allowed** lets a booker bring others. They do not book themselves.
</Accordion>

</Accordions>
