title: "Categories and Tags"
description: "How your catalogue is organised — the difference between the two, and the mistake that makes both useless."
---

Categories and tags both classify what you offer. They are not the same thing, and using
them interchangeably is how a catalogue becomes unusable.

Both live under <Path>Configure > Taxonomy</Path>.

<WhoCan roles="Admin" />

## The difference

| | **Categories** | **Tags** |
|---|---|---|
| What they are | The structure of your catalogue | Keywords |
| How many per course | A few | As many as are true |
| Who uses them | Learners browsing | Learners searching |
| Change often? | Rarely | Freely |

A category answers "what kind of thing is this?". A tag answers "what is it about?".

**Categories** are required on a course. **Tags** are optional.

## Categories

Think of your catalogue's top-level menu. A learner should be able to find roughly what
they want by picking one.

Good: *Business · Languages · IT and Software · Exam Preparation · Health and Care*

<Callout type="warn">
**Too many categories is the usual mistake.** Thirty categories across forty courses is
not a structure; it is a list with extra steps. Fewer, broader categories with tags
underneath work better for everybody.
</Callout>

Fix them early. Changing the category structure once learners have bookmarked pages and
search engines have indexed them costs more than getting it right at the start.

## Tags

Tags are where the detail goes: *beginner · spoken English · IELTS · Excel · evening
batch*.

Add them generously — they cost nothing and they feed
[search](/docs/knowledgebase/search).

## Both appear as filters

A learner browsing your catalogue filters by category, difficulty, language and tags.
That only works if the values are used consistently: "IELTS", "ielts" and "I.E.L.T.S."
are three different tags and one wasted filter.

Agree the names, and have one person responsible for adding new ones.

## Search keywords are a third thing

A course also has **Search Keywords** on its settings step — "other names people might
search for, such as an abbreviation or its name in another language". These are **never
shown to learners**; they only widen what finds the course.

| | Shown to learners | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Category** | Yes | Structure |
| **Tag** | Yes | Topic |
| **Search keyword** | No | Findability |

Put "ইংরেজি" in search keywords, not in tags.

## Common questions

<Accordions type="single">

<Accordion title="Can a course be in more than one category?">
Yes. Do not overdo it — a course in six categories is in none of them, as far as a
browsing learner is concerned.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What happens if I delete a category?">
Courses using it lose it, and a course needs at least one. Move them first.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Do products use the same categories as courses?">
Categories and tags are shared. Products can also have their own
[taxonomies](/docs/knowledgebase/selling-payments/products-catalog) for attributes that
only apply to them.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="We have duplicate tags with different spellings.">
Pick one, re-tag the courses, delete the rest, and write the agreed list down somewhere
your team will see it.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Where do categories appear on the website?">
In your catalogue listings and filters. How they are laid out is part of your site — see
[Pages and blocks](/docs/knowledgebase/website/pages-and-blocks).
</Accordion>

</Accordions>
