Categories and tags are how you organize your content so learners can find what they
want. Categories give you a tidy, structured menu; tags are quick, flexible labels. Most
platforms use both.

## Categories

**Categories** are a structured way to group courses and other content. They can be
**nested**, so you can have a broad category with more specific ones inside it — for
example, **Business → Marketing → Social Media**.

### What you can do

- **Create, edit, and delete** categories.
- **Reorder** them so they appear in the order you want.
- Give each one a **name, description, icon, color, and thumbnail**.
- Set each one to **published, draft, or archived**.
- **Attach** categories to courses and other content.
- See a **count** of how much content sits in each category.
- **Export to a spreadsheet** or **bulk delete** when managing many at once.

### Setting one up

1. Go to **Admin → Categories**.
2. Click **New Category**.
3. Enter a name and, if you like, a description, icon, color, and thumbnail.
4. Choose a parent category to nest it, or leave it at the top level.
5. Set its status and click **Save**.

| Status | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Published** | Live — learners can see and browse it. |
| **Draft** | Hidden from learners while you set it up. |
| **Archived** | Retired but kept — hidden from learners, still visible to you. |

### What the learner sees

Learners browse a **category tree** — an organized menu they can open up to drill into
more specific topics — and they can **filter** what they see by category. Well-named
categories make your catalog feel easy to explore.

## Tags

**Tags** are flat, flexible labels you can stick on many kinds of content — for example
**Beginner-Friendly**, **Live Cohort**, or **Free**. Unlike categories, they aren't
nested; a piece of content can carry several tags at once.

### What you can do

- Create tags and attach them to courses and other content types.
- Use them to highlight qualities that don't fit neatly into a category.

### What the learner sees

Learners can **filter** by tag to quickly narrow down to, say, only beginner-friendly or
only live courses.

<Callout type="info">
A good rule of thumb: use **categories** for the main "where does this belong?" structure,
and **tags** for extra qualities like difficulty, format, or audience.
</Callout>

## Built-in location data

Your platform also includes ready-made **location data** — a complete list of **countries,
states, and cities**. It's used in places like profiles and scheduling, so people can pick
their location from a consistent list. This data is always available and isn't something
you need to set up or edit.
