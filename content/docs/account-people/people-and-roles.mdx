Everyone on your platform has an account. What they can *do* comes from their **role**.

Two separate things decide what somebody sees, and confusing them is the single
commonest support question about staff:

| | Decides |
|---|---|
| **Learner / Instructor / Admin** marks | What kind of person they are — whether they can enrol, whether they can be put on a programme as an instructor. |
| **Role** | What they are actually allowed to open and change. |

Marking somebody as an instructor does not let them do anything. Their role does.

## The people lists

<Path>People</Path> holds five views of the same people:

| View | Who is in it |
|---|---|
| **All Users** | Everyone. |
| **Learners** | People marked as learners. |
| **Instructors** | People marked as instructors. |
| **Admins & Staff** | Your team. |
| **Roles & Permissions** | The roles themselves. |

## Building a role

<Path>People > Roles & Permissions</Path>, then create one.

| Field | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Name** | What the role is called. Required. |
| **Permissions** | What it allows. Search them — "lesson", "quiz.create", "manage". |
| **Status** | **Active** or **Inactive**. |

<Callout type="warn">
**You cannot grant a permission you do not hold yourself.** That is deliberate — it
stops somebody building a role more powerful than their own and then assigning it to
themselves.
</Callout>

Rather than starting from nothing, **clone** a role that is close and adjust it.

## Limiting a role to its own programmes

A role can be switched, in one action, between:

- **Only the programmes they are assigned to** — an instructor sees their own batches.
- **Every programme** — academy-wide.

This applies to every permission in the role that can be limited, at once. It is the
difference between a teacher and a head of department, and it is one click rather than
thirty.

A limited role is also refused a few academy-wide screens — the
[instructor comparison](/docs/knowledgebase/analytics/instructors), for instance, which
needs the whole picture by definition.

## Checking what a role actually opens

**Preview access** shows *"which admin pages this permission set can open right now"*,
and where a page is only partly available it tells you which permissions are missing to
make it whole.

Use it every time you build a role. A role that looks right and opens nothing is the
normal first attempt — a page usually needs the permission to *list* as well as the
permission to *view*.

## Seeing what a role allows, without editing it

**View permissions** on any role opens a read-only view. You do not need permission to
*edit* roles to use it, which matters — "what can this person actually do?" is a question
people ask far more often than they change anything.

It reads in plain words, not in code: **View Courses**, not `course.view`, grouped by the
area each one belongs to. Beside the role it says whether it covers the **Whole academy**
or is **Limited to assigned programmes**.

Super admin is described rather than listed: *"Super admin can do everything in this
academy, including anything added later."*

## Exporting your roles

**Export** gives you one role, or every role, as a spreadsheet — the permission grid,
with each permission named the way the role form names it and grouped by area.

Two things it is genuinely useful for:

- **An audit or an inspection.** "Show us who can change grades" is a download rather
  than a morning of screenshots.
- **Reviewing access with somebody who does not have a login.** A spreadsheet can be read
  by the person who owns the risk, not just by whoever has admin.

## Assigning a role

From a person's record, **Assign Roles**. From the role, its **Members** list and
**Change role** for several people at once.

A person can hold more than one role, and holds the sum of what they allow.

## System roles

Some roles are built in and cannot be deleted. You can see what they hold and assign
them; the platform keeps them intact so an academy cannot lock itself out.

## Roles worth having

| Role | Allowed to |
|---|---|
| **Instructor** | See and teach their own programmes; mark work. |
| **Course builder** | Create and edit content; no access to people or money. |
| **Registrar** | Enrolments, transfers, progression; no content editing. |
| **Finance** | Billing, transactions, refunds; nothing else. |
| **Support** | Read people and enrolments; change very little. |

Start narrow. Widening a role when somebody asks is easy; discovering that everyone has
been able to delete courses is not.

## Common questions

<Accordions type="single">

<Accordion title="A colleague says a page is empty or refuses them.">
Open **Preview access** on their role. The usual cause is holding the permission to view
one thing but not to list them — a list page needs the list permission.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="An instructor cannot see their own course.">
Three things: are they marked as an **Instructor**; does their role allow opening
courses; and are they actually on that programme's instructor or staff list. All three.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="I cannot give somebody a permission I can see.">
You do not hold it yourself. Someone who does has to grant it.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can a role be limited to one batch?">
It can be limited to the programmes a person is assigned to. Put them on that batch and
nothing else, and that is what they see.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What happens if I deactivate a role?">
The people holding it lose what it allowed. Check the role's **Members** first — a
deactivated role with twelve people on it is twelve colleagues locked out.
</Accordion>
