description: "Learners can flag a broken video or a wrong answer from inside the lesson. This is the queue those reports land in."

Under every lesson, module, assignment, file, quiz and quiz question there is a **Report
a problem** link. A learner who finds a video that stops at 2:10, or an answer key that
marks the right answer wrong, can tell you without leaving the page.

The reports land in one queue at <Path>Teach > Reported Problems</Path>.

<WhoCan roles="Admin, Instructor" />

## What a learner sends

The learner picks a reason from a short list and can add a sentence of their own. The
reasons offered depend on what they are reporting:

| Reason | Offered on |
|---|---|
| The content is unclear | Lessons, modules, assignments, quizzes, questions |
| Something is wrong or out of date | Everything |
| A typing or language mistake | Everything |
| An image or visual problem | Quiz questions |
| A video or audio won't play | Lessons |
| A file won't open | Files, assignments |
| Something technical | Everything |
| Something else | Everything |

They are told: *"Tell the course team what's wrong. They'll look at it, and we'll let
you know when it's fixed."* That is a promise the queue exists to keep.

## Working the queue

The list shows **What** was reported, the **Problem** they chose, their own words, the
**Learner**, when it was **Reported**, and its **Status**.

Each report moves through four states:

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **New** | Nobody has looked at it yet. |
| **Being looked at** | Someone has picked it up. |
| **Resolved** | Fixed. The learner is told their report was resolved. |
| **Dismissed** | Not a problem, or not something you will change. |

### The note is for your team, not the learner

When you work a report you can add a **Note for staff** — *"re-uploaded the video"*,
*"fixed the answer key"*. It is kept with the report and shown under its status in the
list, so the next person to open the queue can see what was already done.

<Callout type="warn">
**Only staff see that note. The learner does not.** What the learner gets is the fact
that their report was fixed — not your wording. So write the note for the colleague who
picks this up next week, and if the learner needs an explanation, send them one.
</Callout>

Marking a report **Resolved** is what tells the learner. Dismissing it closes the report
without that message going out, so if a learner has taken the trouble to report
something you are not going to change, it is worth telling them why yourself.

<Callout type="info">
Several learners hitting the same broken video produce several reports. Resolve them
together so everyone who reported it hears back.
</Callout>

## Why it is worth reading

A broken lesson usually produces silence, not reports — learners assume it is their
connection and move on, and the first you hear of it is a refund request. One report is
rarely one learner's problem.

A report about a **quiz question** deserves particular attention: if the answer key is
wrong, every learner who sat that quiz has a wrong mark. Fixing the key does not
re-mark the attempts already taken — see
[Grading quizzes](/docs/knowledgebase/assessments/grading-quizzes) for how to put those
right.

## Common questions

<Accordions type="single">

<Accordion title="I cannot see Reported Problems in the menu.">
It appears for roles that can view course content. If your role cannot open course
content, the item is hidden. See
[People and roles](/docs/knowledgebase/account-people/people-and-roles).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Does the learner find out what happened?">
Yes, when you mark the report **Resolved**. They are told their report was fixed; the
note you leave is for staff and stays internal. Dismissing
a report closes it without that promise being kept, so use a note there too where it
helps.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can learners report anonymously?">
No. Each report carries the learner who sent it, which is what lets you go back to them
if their description is not enough to act on.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="A report says the video will not play, but it plays for me.">
Try it as a learner rather than as an admin — the lesson you open from the builder is
not the lesson they open. If it still plays, the likely causes are the learner's
connection, or a YouTube or Vimeo video the source site has restricted. See
[Video playback](/docs/knowledgebase/files/video-playback).
</Accordion>

</Accordions>
