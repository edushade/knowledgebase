A lesson is a single piece of teaching. You pick its type when you create it, and the
form then asks only for what that type needs.

<WhoCan roles="Admin, Instructor" />

## The seven types

| Type | What it is | What it needs |
|---|---|---|
| **Video** | A video learners watch. | The video, and a **duration**. |
| **Audio** | An audio recording. | The file, and a **duration**. |
| **Text** | A written lesson with formatting, links and images. | Some actual text — an empty editor will not save. |
| **PDF** | A document learners read in the page. | The PDF file. |
| **Live** | A scheduled class. | A **provider**, a **start time** and a **duration**. |
| **Embed** | Content pulled in from somewhere else. | The source, and what it contains. |
| **iFrame** | Another page shown inside the lesson. | The address, and what it contains. |

Every type also takes a **Title**, which is required, and an optional description. Both
can be written in each of your site's languages.

## Video and audio

A video can come from three places:

- **Self hosted** — upload the file and it is served from your own library.
- **YouTube** — paste the link.
- **Vimeo** — paste the link.

**Duration is required**, and it is not decoration: progress is measured against it, so
a wrong duration makes a learner look further along — or further behind — than they are.

How a YouTube or Vimeo video behaves — whether learners can skip ahead, change speed, or
see the source site's branding — is controlled separately. See
[Video playback](/docs/knowledgebase/files/video-playback).

## Live lessons

A **Live** lesson is a class with a time attached. It asks for:

| Field | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Live Session Provider** | **Zoom**, **Google Meet**, **Microsoft Teams**, **Self Hosted**, **Direct URL** or **Other**. Required. |
| **Meeting Starts At** | The date and time. Required. |
| **Duration** | How long it runs. Required. |
| **Meeting Link (Join Link)** | Where learners join. |
| **Meeting Password** | Optional, for meetings that need one. |

<Callout type="info">
Zoom and Google Meet can create the meeting for you once they are connected, instead of
you pasting a link. See [Integrations](/docs/knowledgebase/settings/integrations).
</Callout>

A live lesson is a lesson, not a booking. If you want learners to pick a slot from your
availability, that is [Live sessions and bookings](/docs/knowledgebase/bookings)
instead.

## Embed and iFrame

Both show content that lives elsewhere. The difference is small: an **Embed** takes a
source you paste in, an **iFrame** takes a web address.

Both ask one extra question — **Embed content type**, "Select what this embed contains":

| Answer | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Video** | Treated like a video for completion — watched counts as done. |
| **Audio** | Same as video. |
| **Document (PDF / reading)** | Treated like a reading. |
| **Other** | No assumption made. |

Get this right. It is how the lesson inherits the correct
[completion rule](/docs/knowledgebase/progress-grading/completion-rules); a video
embedded as "Other" will not count itself finished when it is watched.

## Attachments

Any lesson can carry **Attachments** — handouts, slides, datasets — listed under the
lesson for learners to download. Attachments are not separate items in the outline and
do not count towards progress. For a download that *should* be its own item, add a
**File** to the course instead. See
[Files and resources](/docs/knowledgebase/courses/files-and-resources).

## What the learner sees

- **Video** and **audio** play in the page, with their position remembered.
- **Text** reads as a formatted page.
- **PDF** opens in a reader, with full-screen available.
- **Live** shows the scheduled time and a way to join when it is due.
- **Embed** and **iFrame** show the outside content in place.

Under every one of them is a **Report a problem** link, so a learner who hits a broken
video can tell you without leaving the lesson. See
[Reported problems](/docs/knowledgebase/courses/reported-problems).

## Common questions

<Accordions type="single">

<Accordion title="My video lesson will not save.">
Two fields are required and easy to miss: the video itself, and **Duration**. A video
lesson with a duration of zero is refused.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can one lesson hold a video and a reading?">
Not as one lesson. Make the video lesson and the text lesson separately — learners get a
clearer outline and you get honest progress, because each piece can be counted as done
on its own.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Which should I use, Self hosted or YouTube?">
Self hosted keeps the video inside your academy, with no outside branding and no related
videos at the end. YouTube costs you nothing to serve and plays well on weak
connections. If the course is paid, self hosted is usually worth it.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Learners say the video says watched before they have watched it.">
Check the lesson's **Duration** against the real length of the video. Progress is
measured against the duration you typed, so a ten-minute video entered as one minute is
"finished" after sixty seconds.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I change a lesson's type after it is made?">
Yes — open the lesson, pick a different type and supply what the new type needs. The old
content is not carried across, so copy anything you want to keep first.
</Accordion>

</Accordions>
