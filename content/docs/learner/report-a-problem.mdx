Under every lesson, module, assignment, file, quiz and quiz question there is **Report a
problem**.

<WhoCan roles="Learner" />

## Where they find it

Under every item in the course sidebar there is a **⋯** menu, and **Report a problem**
sits in it, just below **Mark as complete**. Anything an enrolled learner can open can
be reported from the same place — a lesson, a file, an assignment, a quiz — so nobody
has to work out where to complain.

## What they see

A short dialog, headed for what they are reporting — "Report a problem with this
lesson", "Report a problem with this quiz", "Report a problem with this file", "Report a
problem with this assignment", "Report a problem with question 3".

Under it: *"Tell the course team what's wrong. They'll look at it, and we'll let you
know when it's fixed."*

They pick a reason:

| Reason |
|---|
| The content is unclear |
| Something is wrong or out of date |
| A typing or language mistake |
| An image or visual problem |
| A video or audio won't play |
| A file won't open |
| Something technical isn't working |
| Something else |

The reasons offered depend on what they are reporting, and they can add a sentence —
the prompt suggests *"the video stops at 2:10"*.

Then **Send report**, and they are told: *"Thanks. We've told the course team, and we'll
let you know when it's fixed."*

## That is a promise

Those two sentences commit you to two things: looking at it, and telling them the
outcome. Both are kept from your
[Reported Problems](/docs/knowledgebase/courses/reported-problems) queue, where
resolving a report with a note is what the learner is shown.

<Callout type="warn">
A queue nobody reads turns that message into a lie, and the learners who told you stop
telling you.
</Callout>

## Why it is worth encouraging

Most learners who hit a broken video assume it is their connection and move on. The
first you hear of it is a refund request, weeks later.

Mention the link in your course instructions. Reports go up immediately, and each one is
a fault you can fix before it costs you a learner.

## What it is not for

Not a support channel. "How do I get my certificate?" is not a content problem, and the
queue is the wrong place for it. Give learners a contact route for that — see
[Forms and leads](/docs/knowledgebase/website/forms-and-leads).

## Common questions

<Accordions type="single">

<Accordion title="Can they report anonymously?">
No. Each report carries the learner, which is what lets you go back to them when the
description is not enough.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can they report during an exam?">
Yes, and it is the right thing to do with a broken question rather than losing time on
it. Say so in your exam instructions.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Do they find out what happened?">
When you mark the report **Resolved**. They are told it was fixed. The note staff leave
on a report is internal — the learner never sees it, so anything they need to know has
to be sent to them.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Nobody reports anything.">
They do not know it is there. Mention it in your course instructions.
</Accordion>

</Accordions>
