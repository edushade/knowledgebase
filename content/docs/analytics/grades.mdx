A distribution of results tells you things an average cannot. This screen shows what
grades are actually being awarded, and where.

<WhoCan roles="Admin, Instructor" />

## What is on it

| View | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **Spread of Results** | Overall grades in bands of ten points. |
| **Grades Awarded** | How many results got each grade, highest first. |
| **At or above the pass mark** | The share of results that passed. |
| **Average**, **Median** | The middle, two ways. |

Average and median together are more useful than either alone. When the average is well
below the median, a small group of very low results is pulling it down — usually
learners who never started and were marked as zero.

## Reading the shape

| Shape | What it usually means |
|---|---|
| A bulge near the pass mark | The pass line is doing its job, or the marking is drifting towards it. |
| Everybody above 90 | The assessments are too easy, or the completion rules are too loose. |
| Two humps | Two groups — often learners who did the work and learners who did not. |
| A spike at zero | Unmarked work counted as zero, not genuine failures. |

<Callout type="warn">
**A spike at zero is usually a setting, not a cohort.** With **Treat Ungraded Items as
Zero** on, every learner with outstanding work is counted at or near zero. Check that
before concluding anything about teaching. See
[Course grading setup](/docs/knowledgebase/progress-grading/course-grading-setup).
</Callout>

## Before you trust the numbers

Three things can make the distribution misleading:

1. **Rounding.** A grade is matched to its band after rounding, so a pass line at 50
   with rounding set to nearest passes everyone on 49.5. See
   [Rounding and precision](/docs/knowledgebase/progress-grading/rounding-and-precision).
2. **Different schemes.** Two programmes on different grading schemes do not share a
   scale. Narrow to one programme before comparing.
3. **Unreleased results.** Held results are not here yet, so a distribution read the day
   after an exam is incomplete.

## What to do about a bad distribution

- **Everyone passing** — look at the quiz analytics per question. Questions everybody
  gets right are not measuring anything. See
  [Quiz analytics](/docs/knowledgebase/assessments/quiz-analytics).
- **Everyone failing one assessment** — check the answer key before you conclude
  anything about the learners. See
  [Question reports](/docs/knowledgebase/assessments/question-reports).
- **A hump at the bottom** — check whether those learners ever started, on
  [Progress](/docs/knowledgebase/analytics/progress).

## Common questions

<Accordions type="single">

<Accordion title="The academy-wide distribution looks meaningless.">
It usually is. Programmes on different schemes and different subjects do not share a
scale. Filter to one programme.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Why is a learner missing from the distribution?">
They have no final result — nothing marked, or their results are held.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="The pass rate here does not match the certificates issued.">
They are different questions. Passing is one of six gates a certificate has to clear.
See [Certificate requirements](/docs/knowledgebase/certificates/certificate-requirements).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I export this?">
Yes. The lists on the analytics screens export.
</Accordion>

</Accordions>
